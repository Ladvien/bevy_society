# Kin: relatedness on a tie, and what a relative gets

`Tie::kin` and `Kin` (`src/relationships.rs`), `Input::Kinship` / `Signal::Kinship` (`src/defs.rs`, `src/behavior.rs`), `Tuning::kin_premium` (`src/core.rs`), `TieSpec::kin` (`src/placement.rs`), `ctx.kin(a, b)` (`src/script.rs`, `src/snapshot.rs`), `TieSave::kin` (`src/save.rs`).

## The problem

`relationships.md` listed, under what the model leaves out, "kin, role or structural ties (family, employer); only interaction history counts". A society of people with no families is the one thing a host cannot build with this crate: a mother, a brother and a cousin are, to the crate, three strangers with whatever affinity the author typed in. The only workaround in the code is a comment on `Tuning::bereavement_familiarity`: "it exists so a **family member** grieves: they are spawned with high familiarity and no time to reach the affinity a `Friend` label needs". A host cannot say who is related to whom, a pack cannot score by it, a script cannot ask it, and a Dunbar-style trim of a long-neglected tie would forget a sister.

## Sources

Read for this note, in the order they are used. "Record" means the title, DOI and date were returned by `paper_get` / `paper_search` and the abstract was not available.

- **Hamilton 1964**, *The genetical evolution of social behaviour. I.*, Journal of Theoretical Biology 7(1):1-16, DOI `10.1016/0022-5193(64)90038-4` (record). The coefficient of relatedness `r` is the probability that two people share a given gene by common descent: `0.5` for a parent and a child and for full siblings, `0.25` for a grandparent, an aunt or uncle, a niece or nephew and a half-sibling, `0.125` for a first cousin. These are the numbers the `Kin` enum carries. They are not a rate and not a design choice; the paper's rule (help is favoured when `r · benefit > cost`) is why `r` is the right scale for how much a relative gets.
- **Curry, Roberts & Dunbar 2013**, *Altruism in social networks: Evidence for a 'kinship premium'*, British Journal of Psychology 104(2):283-295, DOI `10.1111/j.2044-8295.2012.02119.x` (abstract read). 111 participants rated willingness to be altruistic and emotional closeness toward 12 family members and friends at different positions in their networks. "Altruism was greater for family than friends, and greater for more central layers of the network. Crucially, the results showed that kinship made a significant unique contribution to altruism, even when controlling for the effects of emotional closeness. Thus, participants were more altruistic towards kin than would be expected if altruism was dependent on emotional closeness alone - a phenomenon we label a 'kinship premium'." This is the mechanism `kin_premium` implements: a relative's gift is chosen *beyond* what the affinity the giver holds for them would give. It supplies the existence and the direction of the premium and no size.
- **Burton-Chellew & Dunbar 2015**, *Hamilton's rule predicts anticipated social support in humans*, Behavioral Ecology 26(1):130-137, DOI `10.1093/beheco/aru165` (abstract read). 540 people ranked the members of their support network by who would be most likely to help: "relatives were more likely to be ranked in primary positions and ... the degree of relatedness correlated with rank." The premium therefore grows with `r` (a cousin gets less than a sibling), which is why the model multiplies by `2r` and not by a flag.
- **Cassar & Friedman 2026** (working paper), *Hamilton's Rule for Humans: Cooperative Breeding with a Role for Reciprocity*, SSRN, DOI `10.2139/ssrn.6460000` (abstract read; not peer reviewed): "transfers to recipients increase with their genetic relatedness to the donor" in dictator games and child-care networks in three societies, "cultural norms shape cooperation, but do not trump relatedness". Used only as a second voice for an increasing, not saturating, dependence on `r`.
- **Prigerson et al. 2009** (already cited by the crate), DOI `10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121`: grief follows the loss of a close relationship. A relative is close by the relation, so an authored relative is a close tie whatever its label or familiarity (`absence::closeness`).

**Not used, and why.** Roberts & Dunbar 2011, *The costs of family and friends: an 18-month longitudinal study of relationship maintenance and decay*, Evolution and Human Behavior 32(3):186-197, DOI `10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.08.005` (record only: `paper_get` returns no abstract, `openalex_get` has no entry, `paper_download` finds no open copy). Its title is the question a kin model invites (are family ties kept alive by contact the way friendships are?) and the crate does not answer it: a tie of a relative is exempt from the Dunbar trim, and nothing else about decay differs between kin and non-kin, because the finding was not read.

## The model

- **`Tie::kin` is `Option<Kin>`**: what the other person is to the owner, `None` for a stranger to the family. It is on the directed tie like every other tie fact; relatedness `r` is symmetric, so the two directions carry converse names (`Parent` answered by `Child`, `AuntUncle` by `NieceNephew`, `Sibling` by `Sibling`) and the same `r`. `Kin` is a closed list of the relations with a published `r`: `Parent`, `Child`, `Sibling` (0.5), `HalfSibling`, `Grandparent`, `Grandchild`, `AuntUncle`, `NieceNephew` (0.25), `Cousin` (0.125). `Kin::relatedness()` and `Tie::relatedness()` return `r`; a name and not just a number is kept so a host can show "mother" and the editor's round trip loses nothing.
- **`Input::Kinship`** reads `(2r).clamp(0, 1)` for a `Person` target: `1.0` for a first-degree relative, `0.5` for a grandparent, `0.25` for a cousin, `0` for anyone without a kin tie, with or without a tie at all. The doubling is a scale choice so that a first-degree relative fills the input; a pack curve rescales it.
- **`Tuning::kin_premium`** (default `1.0`): an activity that **gives goods and takes none** (its interaction's `actor_gives` is not empty, `target_gives` is) scores `score · (1 + kin_premium · 2r)` for a relative, **where the giver holds more than the receiver by more than `Tuning::trade_reserve`** in every good the gift hands over. The multiply is applied to the option's own score like `crowding_penalty`, so it changes which person the giver picks (the best target) and how strongly the giving activity competes with the rest of the day, and it touches nothing for a society with no relatives. `0.0` is the previous release. **The size is a design default**: the three sources give the direction and the dependence on `r`, and no number.
- **Why "where giving helps".** `value_of` is `1 / (1 + held)` and a unit moves whole. A unit handed from `m` to `t` is a welfare gain for the pair only if `m − t` exceeds the unit; at `m − t` equal to the unit it only swaps the two shelves (1.8 and 0.8 become 0.8 and 1.8), and the receiver is then the one with a surplus. The first version of the premium multiplied every gift to a relative; **8.5-24.5 % of accepted shares were then a loaf returned within three hours** (1.7 % with no premium; `docs/loop/runs/2026-10-31-05/eval.md`), since a relative is chosen again and again. A premium for altruism is not owed to a swap, so it is withheld there, and the swaps that the shipped `Surplus` input still allows between strangers are a separate, older defect (below, "Not modelled").
- **A relative is never trimmed.** `Ties::trim_keeping` treats a tie with `kin` set as frozen, the way a dead person's tie is, so a Dunbar cap cannot delete the one record of who someone's mother is.
- **A relative is close.** `absence::closeness` (used by bereavement, by missing someone away, and by the reunion) counts a kin tie as close whatever its label and familiarity; the intensity is still `0.5 · (1 + affinity)`, so a relative the person dislikes grieves less, which is what the crate already says about everyone else.
- **Authoring.** `TieSpec::kin: Option<Kin>` (default `None`). Spawning creates the other person's tie when they authored none (neutral affinity, trust 0.5), gives it the converse relation, and gives both a familiarity of `1.0` (a relative is someone you have always known). Two authored directions that are not converses, or a `kin` on a tie to oneself, are refused with both people named.
- **Scripts** read `ctx.kin(a, b)`, `r` in `[0, 1]`, `0` with no tie.
- **Saves** carry `TieSave::kin` with `#[serde(default)]`: a save from the previous release loads every tie as no relation.

## What is not modelled

- **Roles and structural ties other than blood** (employer, spouse, in-laws, godparents). A spouse has `r = 0` and is the pairing question of `docs/loop/needs-decision/romance-and-pairing.md`; this change makes the dyad container that decision lacked (a tie fact both directions carry) available but adds no pairing.
- **Births and ageing.** A child is spawned by the host with the relatives it names; the crate does not make people.
- **Kin recognition under uncertainty, nepotism in witness reactions, inheritance, kin forgiveness.** Each has a direction in the literature and none was read for this change.
- **Whether contact keeps a family tie alive** (above).
- **A different premium for a different kind of giving.** Only the goods an interaction hands over are favoured; a compliment or help in kind is not altruism the crate counts.
- **A loaf passed back between strangers.** The `Surplus` input lets a unit move when the receiver holds exactly one unit less than the giver (`gap >= trade_margin` only reads the value gap before the move), so two people holding 1.8 and 0.8 can hand the same loaf back and forth. Measured on the shipped village with no relatives: 1.7-3.1 % of accepted shares are returned within three hours (`docs/loop/runs/2026-10-31-05/evidence/`). Requiring that the giver hold more than the receiver by more than `trade_reserve` would remove it; it changes every trade and every protected economy check, so it is recorded as the next item and not made here.
