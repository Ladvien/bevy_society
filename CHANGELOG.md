### Changed

- **A relationship is now mutual: the dyad got the balance step the crate never had.** Heider's balance theory is usually quoted for triads, and it is just as much a statement about a dyad — "in a dyadic relation, balance basically means reciprocity: **feeling positive about a person who doesn't reciprocate this feeling is unbalanced**" (via Schweighofer & Schweitzer, DOI `10.18564/jasss.5323`). `balance_triads` implemented the triadic half; nothing implemented the dyadic one. Measured on `village-evening` (16.4 game-days): **27%** of directed friend ties unreciprocated and the two directions of a pair disagreeing by **0.133**, against a `friend_band` of 0.05. It reaches behaviour because the decision model reads the *actor's* tie (`Signal::Affinity`) while the acceptance draw reads the *target's*.

  `Tuning::dyad_balance_per_day` (0.5) pulls a pair's two directed affinities toward their mean, both directions moving so the mean is conserved. Measured: gap **0.133 → 0.048** (seed 7), **0.123 → 0.046** (42), **0.130 → 0.050** (11). **The cost is real and stated**: two people who disagree cannot be one friend and one rival, so rivals fall 42 → 24 at seed 7 and 10-18 on every seed, and seed 7's friends fall 22 → 16.

  **Two implementations were measured and rejected before this one shipped.** Every unordered pair is `O(n^2)` and took the 5 000-person benchmark from **495.7 to 942.0 ms** a tick. `Perception::contact` was affordable but wrong — only people within `contact_radius` were balanced, closing the gap to 0.069 instead of 0.048. The shipped version iterates each person's **tie list**, which is exactly the pairs that have a relationship and is capped by `max_ties`: identical results to all pairs, **507.9 ms** at 5 000 people (+2.5%, inside the spread). A separate prediction — that this would lower the refusal rate — was **wrong** and is recorded: refusals stay at 35.2%, because the acceptance draw is dominated by `accept.base` 0.50 (mean P 0.656, mean affinity term only +0.058).

And the README promised "**rivalries red**" while no clip has ever drawn one: 3 800 steps of settle inside a 32 s game-day is six game-hours, far too short for a rivalry, and widening the settle to 9 000 and 8 000 still read **0**. The full 16-day run does form them (24, `became_rival` 271), so the caption now says what the clip shows and `docs/DEMOS.md` records the limit.

Measured on `village-evening` (seed 7, 24 people, 16.4 game-days): **523** friendships made and **504** lost, held a median of **0.36 game-days**. `friend_affinity` 0.30 against `affinity_ceiling` 0.35 puts a friendship in a **0.05-wide shelf** while a median tie's affinity swings **0.072**, **7%** of positive tie-samples sit inside the shelf, and **91%** of the endings fail the affinity gate with the affinity at the moment of ending clustering on exactly **0.25** and **0.30** — one `-0.05` step, which is `Tuning::rejection_affinity_penalty`, paid by whoever's approach was refused at a **38%** refusal rate.

  **Measured effect** (seed 7; seed 42 and 11 agree in direction): total label messages **1 953 → 1 546** (−21%), friendships that end **504 → 242**, median held **0.36 → 1.01 game-days**, **rivals unchanged at 42** and `became_rival` unchanged at 272, rumor reach and interrupted endings identical. Holding the friend bar *unconditionally* instead — the option rejected last iteration — took rivals to **48**; the band is what makes the difference possible.

  **What it does not fix, and says so.** Endings with **no interaction behind them** fall only **56 → 43**: the band absorbs shallow dips, and telling a quiet friendship from one whose owner was refused needs a "last exchange" stamp that `Tie` does not have (`last_contact_day` is stamped on mere proximity). That is a `SocietySave::VERSION` bump, recorded in `docs/loop/needs-decision/which-tie-field-says-someone-spoke.md`. Mechanism: Altman & Taylor 1973's staged, *maintained* relationships, via Battle & White 2024 (corpus `10.33790_jmhsb1100188`); the width is a design default chosen by measurement.

Measured on `village-evening` (seed 7, 24 people, 16.4 game-days) before the change: **2 867** `TieLabelChanged` messages, one every **8.2 game-minutes**, with 85 of the 276 ordered pairs changing more than ten times on their own. Half were events and half were noise — `Stranger -> Acquaintance` fired **828** times with a median margin of **0.0027** from its bar and a one-tick step of **0.0002**, while `Friend <-> Acquaintance` fired 1 027 times with a one-tick step of **0.013**, which is one accepted `compliment` or one witnessed deed and *should* change the label.

  **The band is on the stranger bar only, and that is the measured answer.** Applied to every bar it cut messages 35% but took rivals 42 → **48** and friends 18 → **20** at seed 7 — it held friendships past the event that ended them, a lie in the other direction. - **The starter pack contradicts the paper it cites: good loses to bad on numbers, and by total weight too.** `snub`'s consideration cites **Baumeister, Bratslavsky, Finkenauer & Vohs 2001**, *Bad Is Stronger Than Good* (DOI `10.1037/1089-2680.5.4.323`), which makes **two** claims: "bad impressions ... are quicker to form and **more resistant to disconfirmation** than good ones", *and* "**good may prevail over bad by superior force of numbers**: Many good events can overcome the psychological effects of a single bad one". The crate honours the first and violates the second. Measured on `village-evening`, seed 7, 24 people over 17 game-days, summing every affinity write: **1 604** accepted `compliment`s moved affinity by **+160.4** in total (**+0.10** each), while an `insult` (deed harm `-0.4`) reached witnesses **7 920** times and moved them by **-241.2** in total, a mean of **-0.0305** per witness. Bad is ahead **1.5:1** in total weight, and good is outnumbered **4.9:1** by events: a compliment weighs about 3.3x one witness's reaction, but there are 4.9x as many reactions. Good loses on numbers, not on weight. **An earlier version of this entry was wrong:** it multiplied `witness_gain` (0.2) by the insult's harm (-0.4) and called the product `-0.08` of affinity per witness, a 4.8:1 gap per event. `witness_gain` moves **trust**; affinity moves by `0.15 x approval` in `src/reputation.rs`, which is where the `-0.0305` comes from. The ledger is reproducible: `cargo run --release --example evidence -- --scenario village-evening --seed 7 --out <dir>` writes `accepted_compliments` to `summary.json`, and the insult side is the sum of the `0.15 * approval` writes where `approval < 0.0`. Friendship cannot accumulate and the village settles at **18 friend pairs against 42 rival pairs**. **Nothing changed, deliberately.** The obvious implementation — making negative affinity decay more slowly — was built and measured and it **doubles** rivalries (**42 -> 82**; still 64 with structural balance disabled, so balance explains only 18 of the 40): persisting grudges persist while friendship keeps fading at the same rate, so the counterweight does not grow. A stronger `compliment` (+0.10 -> +0.20) was inconsistent across seeds (31 rivals / 14 friends, then 44 / 7). The shape the paper actually prescribes — raise the **rate** of friendly interaction, not its weight — is new authored content and needs a three-axis measurement (ties, needs, interruptions) first, so it is recorded with three options in `docs/loop/needs-decision/good-is-outweighed-by-bad.md` rather than shipped. `docs/research/relationships.md` now carries the citation, both of its claims, and the arithmetic.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-25

### Added

- **`summary.json` now reports whether the village ever makes up, not just how many rivalries exist.** `rivals` alone cannot distinguish a society that holds grudges from one that never releases them — both show a rising count. `examples/evidence.rs` now drains `TieLabelChanged` and reports `became_rival` and `left_rival`, whose difference is exactly the standing ties: `became_rival - left_rival == 2 * rivals`. Measured on `village-evening` (24 people, 17 game-days):

  | seed | became rival | left rival | ratio | final rivals | friends |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 7 | 272 | **188** | 1.45 | 42 | 18 |
  | 42 | 308 | **238** | 1.29 | 35 | 19 |
  | 11 | 377 | **297** | 1.27 | 40 | 14 |

  **The village does reconcile** — roughly a quarter of rivalries end — but it forms them 1.3-1.45x faster than it dissolves them and settles at **15% of all pairs being rivals against 6.5% being friends**. The count decelerates (second-half growth 0.68x the first), so it saturates rather than running away. **No `Tuning` value ships with this, deliberately:** the only lever, `affinity_relax_per_day`, moves rivals and friends *together* — 0.01 gives 42 rivals and 18 friends, 0.02 gives 25 and 7, 0.03 gives **10 and 5** — because relaxation pulls all affinity toward zero and cannot tell a grudge from a fondness. Choosing a point is choosing a village's temperament, so it is recorded with three options in `docs/loop/needs-decision/what-does-the-village-owe-its-grudges.md`; the structural fix is separate forgetting rates for positive and negative affinity, which would need a cited default rather than a reparameterisation. `tests/reconciliation.rs` pins the identity (a swapped counter reads −84 against +84) and that reconciliation exists at all. A first version of that test asserted the ratio responds to `affinity_relax_per_day`; it passed at both 0.0 and 0.02, so it was **deleted rather than kept as false coverage**.

- **A crowded place admits its own residents, and the clamp is what made that affordable.** Everyone goes to bed at the same hour, so every resident of a home aims at the same coordinate inside `interaction_radius`; ORCA holds two people's centres one body diameter apart, so the disc packs solid and the queue behind it expires its whole `travel_timeout_hours`. Measured: at every timed-out `sleep` in `village-evening` the goal already held a **median of 6 bodies**, and a 1.5 m disc seats only about `(1.5 / 0.7)^2 = 4.6`. New `Tuning::crowded_arrival_growth` (0.7) grows a `Place`'s accepted arrival boundary by one body diameter per `sqrt(occupants)` — the square root because a crowd occupies *area*. Measured on three seeds, same village, 17 game-days:

  | seed | interrupted | `sleep` | friends | rivals |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 7 | 401 -> **329** (-18%) | 169 -> **129** (-24%) | 15 -> 18 | 28 -> 42 |
  | 42 | 417 -> **369** (-12%) | 176 -> **139** (-21%) | 19 -> 14 | 25 -> 35 |
  | 11 | 438 -> **383** (-13%) | 187 -> **148** (-21%) | 14 -> 9 | 30 -> 40 |

  **This is the same fix that was rejected two iterations ago.** It was measured then at **38 -> 61 rivals** and recorded as a decision for the host; `Tuning::affinity_ceiling` (merged since) bounds that drift, and the same change now costs 10-14 rivalries against a baseline that has already halved friendships. The earlier blocker was correct when written and had expired — re-measuring before building is why this shipped instead of a third warning-only iteration. `crowded_arrival_growth = 0.0` reproduces the old arrival test **exactly** (401 / 169 / 15 / 28), so a host can turn it off with one number, and a test asserts that an empty disc still refuses a walker at 2.2 m. `tests/crowded_arrival.rs` asserts the gate directly: four end-to-end fixtures were built first and **all produced identical numbers with the feature on and off**, because a single crowded disc does not reproduce what four homes and a shared gathering place do together. The shipped test uses a stationary walker with **no `Destination`** — with one, `walk_toward_destination` walks them in and the test passes with the gate disabled, which is how the earlier attempts failed silently.

- **The crate names a home that cannot seat its residents, which is what interrupts 150 sleeps in its own village.** Everyone goes to bed at the same hour, so every resident of a place aims at the same coordinate inside `interaction_radius`; ORCA holds two people one body diameter apart, so the disc packs solid and the queue behind it times out. Instrumenting the arrival gate found the mechanism: at every one of the **150** timed-out sleeps the goal already held a **median of 6 bodies, maximum 10** — and those homes declare `capacity: 6`, while a 1.5 m disc seats only about `(1.5 / 0.7)² ≈ 4.6`. The failures are not lost walkers; they are the queue behind a full disc. `warn_unreachable_places` — already a `Startup` system measuring the layout — now counts residents per home and says so:

  ```
  'well' is home to 8 people, but an arrival disc of radius 1.5 seats about 4. They all go to bed at the same
  hour, so the queue behind the disc times out and their sleeps are interrupted. Give them more room: raise
  `interaction_radius` to about 2.0 or spread the residents over more homes.
  ```

  **No fix lever ships, and that is deliberate.** Three more were built and measured — a per-person crowd allowance at 0.35 m and 0.70 m, and a physical gate of `radius + body * sqrt(occupants)` — and every one trades interruptions for rivalries: **400/38 -> 349/59, 290/51, 316/61**. Interruptions fall 21-28% and rivalries rise 13-23% every time, because the mechanism is **time in contact**, not contact radius (4.0 m, against a ~2 m arrival disc): arriving earlier means the activity starts earlier and spends its full duration standing still instead of walking. That is a real trade, and which side of it a host prefers is **the host's decision**, so the crate reports the numbers instead of choosing silently. The seat count is stated as a **lower bound**: ORCA queues at the centre rather than packing around it, so hex packing understates the crowding, and the warning says "about" rather than pretending to simulate. `tests/crowded_home.rs` asserts both halves — fires on a twelve-resident home, silent on a two-resident one — in one test, because `tracing` allows a single global subscriber per process.

### Changed

- **Ties stay discriminating: affinity is clamped, and `Friend` needs trust.** Measured on a 60-person village over 30 game-days, seed 7: **155 friend ties of 3 540 ordered pairs (4.4%) and 656 rivals**, against a goal of under 25% friends, at least 6 of them, and at least one rival. Six earlier mechanisms were measured and did not work — the threshold alone never discriminated because affinity reached 1.0 and passed every bar, a saturating interaction term was nearly neutral, a trust gate alone did nothing, a contact-triggered exchange turned the village into a clique, and a lower `mere_exposure_per_day` moved friends only 22 → 16. The cause was a saturating system with a **high asymptote**, so the lever is the asymptote:
  - `Tuning::affinity_ceiling` (0.35) bounds `|affinity|`, and `Tie::add_affinity(delta, tuning)` is now the **only** way affinity moves, so no call site can escape it. `rival_affinity` drops 0.4 → 0.30 and `friend_affinity` 0.4 → 0.30, because both sat **above** the ceiling and were therefore unreachable — measured, 0.4 gave 155 friends and **0 rivals**, so the network was one-sided.
  - `TieLabel::classify_with_trust` adds the gate `Friend` was always missing: trust separates friend-ties from the rest **sevenfold** (0.480 against 0.068) while familiarity does not separate them at all (0.907 against 0.908). `Tuning::friend_trust` is 0.5.
  - `Tuning::affinity_weight(affinity)` returns affinity divided by the ceiling, and the Deffuant and conformity weights read it, so the opinion model keeps the scale it was tuned against instead of reading at most a third of it. `AcceptDef` keeps raw affinity, as the measured configuration does.
  - The starter pack's `compliment` gate moves from `Logistic(8.0, 0.7)` to `Logistic(16.0, 0.25)`, which is where the clamp's ceiling puts the affinity a compliment needs. Measured, mood improves to **-0.045** against the **-0.054** baseline that had no clamp at all.
  - `tests/opinion_convergence.rs`'s bar moves from a third to a fifth of the spread: the clamp makes every opinion weight smaller, so the same run now settles at **0.3967** from **0.5106** (22.3%) where it used to reach 0.3699 (27.6%), and it asymptotes rather than continuing to fall. `docs/loop/scenarios.yaml` restates the observable to the measured value; the no-collapse half is unchanged.
  - `examples/evidence.rs` gains `--days`, and `tests/common/village.rs` now holds the village `tests/determinism.rs` used to keep to itself so the bound test and the determinism test cannot drift.

- **`BoundPlaces` and `ActivityTarget::Bound`: each person can be sent to *their* place, not the nearest place of that kind.** Sanctuary assigns each townsperson their own work site each morning — the woodlot, the quarry, a stretch of wall — and `Place(tag)` picks the nearest place with the tag for everyone, while `Home` is exactly one place per person. `BoundPlaces(Vec<(String, Entity)>)` is `Home` generalised to any number of named places, `bind`/`get` read and replace them, and `ActivityTarget::Bound(name)` compiles to the place that person has bound under that name. A person with **no** binding gets no candidate and simply does not do the activity — and is not warned about, because a host that binds some people and not others (a night watch has no work place) would otherwise be told once per person for nothing. A binding to something that is not a `Place`, or to one at capacity, gets no candidate and **is** warned about once per `(PersonId, name)`, because that is a wiring mistake. `Distance` is a valid consideration for a `Bound` target. `tests/bound_places.rs` pins the issue's three criteria.

- **`ReportDeed`: a host can report a deed its own rules saw, naming the witnesses, and `RumorLearned` now carries the teller.** Sanctuary resolves events the society has no eyes for — a townsperson found afflicted at dawn, a person accused by a crowd at the well — and they must enter reputation and rumor **as seen by the people the game names** (the family, the work crew, the crowd), then spread person to person. A deed that enters through an accepted `Interact` gets its witnesses from `Perception`; `ReportDeed { actor, patient, deed, witnesses }` is the same path with the list supplied, so each named witness gets the trust change and a `Witnessed` memory and no other person does, and a deed over `rumor_threshold` creates a rumor known by exactly those witnesses. The witnesses are deduped and ordered by `PersonId` before the deed is reported, so a host's `Vec` order cannot change the society; the actor and the patient are never witnesses of their own deed, a witness that is not a person is dropped with one warning, and an empty list means no deed at all. `RumorLearned` gains `from: Option<Entity>` — `Some(teller)` through gossip, `None` when the listener witnessed the deed or the host planted the rumor — because what a listener makes of a rumor depends on who told them. `witness()` became `witness_deed(.., witnesses: &[Entity])`, the one path both entry points share. `tests/report_deed.rs` pins the issue's three criteria plus the ordering, the party exclusion, an unknown deed and an empty witness list.

### Fixed

- **The guide's scale curve was wrong by 2x, and the benchmark could not have told anyone.** `docs/guide/concepts.md` claimed **13.5 ms** per society tick at 1 000 people and **245.1 ms** at 5 000. `examples/benchmark` timed a **single window** and printed a mean, so on a shared machine it measured the load rather than the code — and measured, the variance is enormous:

  | persons | fastest window | median of 9 windows | spread |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 100 | **0.74 ms** | 1.08 | 103% |
  | 1 000 | **28.4 ms** | 60.4 | **100%** |
  | 2 000 | **100.6 ms** | 140.1 | 49% |

  A **100% spread at 1 000 people**. The figure that *is* reproducible is the **fastest window**: 28.29 / 28.38 / 28.38 / 28.48 ms across four separate invocations (**0.7%**), and 100.55 / 100.93 at 2 000 (**0.4%**) — while the medians from the same runs moved 18-25%. A fast window is evidence the machine was momentarily quiet and a slow one is evidence it was not, so the minimum bounds what the code costs and the median bounds what the machine costs. The guide was answering the second and presenting it as the first. `examples/benchmark` now takes `--reps N` and prints `ms_per_tick_min`, `ms_per_tick_median`, `ms_per_tick_max` and `spread_pct`; the guide's table is replaced with the measured figures (0.74 / **28.4** / 100.6 ms), states the method, treats `spread_pct` above ~20 as "this machine was too busy for the figure to mean anything", and **retracts the old numbers explicitly** with the reason they survived nine iterations. The 5 000-person figure is removed rather than replaced: it does not complete at `--reps 7 --ticks 300` inside a 30-minute deadline here, so the guide's 245.1 ms is unreachable and no longer quoted. `tests/benchmark_reporting.rs` pins the contract — it parses the tool's own JSON, asserts min ≤ median ≤ max, and **recomputes `spread_pct` from the reported windows** so a tool that printed a spread it did not measure would fail.

- **The village existed three times, and one copy had drifted — so three tests were asserting things about a village nobody measures.** `examples/common/scene.rs` is canonical (the capture frames from it), `examples/evidence.rs` mirrored it with a comment promising agreement, and `tests/common/village.rs` held a third copy that had moved: tavern `(8.0, -6.0)` against `(6.5, -5.0)`, market `(-8.0, -6.0)` against `(-6.5, -5.0)`, workshop `(8.0, 8.0)` against `(6.5, 5.0)`. The farthest place pair was therefore **21.3 m** in the tests against **16.4 m** in the measured village — 29% wider. At the `village-evening` scenario's 64 s/day a 16.4 m span needs `travel_timeout_hours` **4.4** and a 21.3 m span needs **5.7**, against a declared 4.0, so a test on the drifted layout was exercising walks no measured run or clip has ever made. `tests/determinism.rs`, `tests/friendship_bound.rs` and `tests/save.rs` all used it and all **passed**, which is why nobody noticed: a passing test on the wrong village is worse than a failing one on the right village. The test builder now matches the canonical coordinates; `examples/evidence.rs` no longer keeps a copy and reads `common::scene::PLACES[..].1` directly (same crate tree — a comment was the only thing keeping them equal, and a comment is not a mechanism), with `series.csv` **byte-identical** before and after; and `tests/village_layout.rs` pins the canonical table, the 16.4 m span and the shared builder's agreement, failing with both coordinates named if they drift again. **The recorded top item was wrong for four iterations** — it read "move the builder where `tests/` can reach it", but the builder had already been extracted and the fixtures that failed last iteration failed because of this geometry mismatch, not because anything was unreachable.

- **The commotion channel was never written, so `FieldInput::Commotion` always scored 0 and a `Gradient(Commotion, _)` target never had a direction — while the module's own doc said a witnessed deed deposited `|harm|`.** Two halves. (1) A witnessed deed now deposits `|harm|` at the actor's cell, which is what the doc promised and what a `gawk` activity needs. (2) `Commotion` is public and takes a **plane position** rather than an actor entity — `Commotion { at: Vec2, amount: f32 }` — so a host can raise trouble the society cannot see (a townsperson found afflicted at dawn, a scuffle at the well) without naming a person. `SocialFields::world_to_cell` maps the point, so a host needs no grid geometry. A negative or non-finite amount deposits nothing: a commotion is a positive disturbance, and a non-positive deposit would read back as absence while still costing a deposit walk. `tests/commotion.rs` pins the issue's three criteria and the rejections.

### Added

- **`Deceased`: death keeps the social graph, so a town can grieve and a dead face can come back.** Despawning a person ran `on_person_removed`, which deletes every tie to and from them and retires rumours about them, so there was no way to mark a death at all — and Sanctuary needs both halves: its dead stay on the roster (a townsperson killed at night can return as one of the walking dead under their own name, and the people who knew them falter), and the town grieves. `Deceased` implies everything `Dormant` does and **freezes the ties that point at them and the ties they hold**: `relax_affinity`, `balance_triads`, `trim_ties` and `refresh_labels` all skip a tie whose holder or target is dead, and `Ties::trim_keeping` excludes a frozen tie from the Dunbar count rather than trading it away, so a town that has lost half its people keeps every one of them. Inserting it writes `PersonDied` **once**, fires `TriggerOn::PersonDied` with the deceased bound as the actor, and makes every living person whose tie is a `Friend` — or whose `familiarity_peak` reaches the new `Tuning::bereavement_familiarity` (0.3), so a **family member** grieves without ever having had time to become a friend — feel `Appraise(Distress)` at `(0.5 · (1 + affinity)).clamp(0, 1)` plus a `Custom("bereaved")` memory. The intensity is proportional to closeness because Prigerson et al. 2009 (DOI `10.1371/journal.pmed.1000121`) key prolonged grief disorder to the loss of a close relationship rather than to any death. `tests/deceased.rs` pins the issue's four criteria plus the trigger.

- **`Signals`: a per-person value the host owns, readable by considerations and by Rhai, for the facts a need is the wrong carrier for.** A need decays and an activity pays it, so a game using one as a flag ("has a day job today", "on the night watch", "grieving", "afflicted") watches the flag fade and makes whatever pays that need look like the cure. A `signals: [(id: "day_job")]` list interns `SignalId`s exactly as `needs` does, `Person` now requires `Signals(pub Vec<f32>)` sized by `size_person_state` to `defs.signals.len()` filled with `0.0`, and **the crate never writes a value**: `Signals::set_id(id, v)` and `Signals::set(defs, "name", v)` are the host's, a value never written reads `0.0` and a non-finite write stores `0.0`. `Input::Signal(String)` compiles to `Signal::Host(SignalId)` and scores through the decided actor's value, and `ctx.signal(a, id)` exposes it to scripts off the same tick-start snapshot `ctx.need` uses. **An undeclared signal is a load-time `DefsError::UnknownId` with the definition's path** — `activity 'work' consideration 0` — never a silent 0. Pinned by `tests/signals.rs`: a `Signal("x")` through `Step(0.5)` scores nothing at `0` (the person idles, the trace carries `signals: [0.0]`) and exactly `1.0` at `1` (the activity is taken); `ctx.signal(actor, "x")` in a `Script` consideration sees a host write made after the previous tick, so clearing `x` between two ticks leaves the next decision's `work` unscored in one frame; and `Signal("day_job")` with no `day_job` declared fails the load with `UnknownId { kind: "signal", id: "day_job", owner: "activity 'work' consideration 0" }`.

- **The crate can now tell whether its own layout is walkable, and name the pair it cannot reach.** `warn_short_travel_reach` computes how many metres `travel_timeout_hours` buys, but its own doc comment admitted the limit: *"above it the crate cannot tell, because it does not know the layout"* — and `finish()` genuinely cannot, having no world yet. A `Startup` system can: every `Place` carries a `Transform`, so `warn_unreachable_places` measures every pairwise distance and warns when the timeout does not cover the farthest pair. Measured on the project's own `village-evening`, where the old warning stayed silent because 14.9 m is above its 10 m floor:

  ```
  a travel_timeout_hours of 4 at seconds_per_day 64 buys 14.9 metres of walking, but the farthest pair of
  places is 'market' -> 'workshop' at 16.4 m. Every walk between them times out before arrival, so the
  activities that need one end interrupted and the needs they satisfy are never met. Raise
  travel_timeout_hours to at least 4.4, or move the places closer.
  ```

  Reachable distance is `speed * seconds_per_day * timeout_hours / 24`, so 4 game-hours at 64 s/day is **14.9 m** against a **16.4 m** worst case — short by 1.5 m. **The honest accounting: this explains only 8 of the village's 150 `sleep` failures.** Raising the harness to 5 h silences the warning and moves interruptions 400 -> 312, but `sleep` only 150 -> 142; the surviving 142 time out with an 18.6 m budget against a 16.4 m worst case, so they are not reachability — they are walkers who never complete a straight line, a separate defect recorded rather than claimed. Instrumenting all four exit paths for `sleep` first showed **150 of 150** on `timed_out=true`, `gone=false`, `suspended` and `lost` never firing, which is what made the reachability question the right one to ask. Skipped under `Locomotion::Host` and when there is no timeout, the same guards the neighbouring warning uses. `tests/unreachable_places.rs` asserts both halves — fires at 30 m, silent at 5 m — in **one** test, because `tracing` allows a single global subscriber per process and a second test in the same binary would read an empty string.

- **`Dormant`: take a person out of their daily life without destroying who they are, and `EndActivity` to cancel one activity without dormancy.** Sanctuary posts a townsperson on the wall for a night, shelters them indoors, sends them on an expedition or puts them in a conversation with the player; removing `Autonomous` stopped new decisions but left the running `Doing` travelling and still targetable as a `Person` partner, and removing `Person` ran `on_person_removed`, which deletes every tie to and from them. `Dormant` is the state in between. A dormant person keeps their ties, memories, opinions, knowledge and memberships — needs keep decaying and emotions keep relaxing, because time still passes for them — while the crate stops choosing activities for them, keeps them out of every other person's `Perception::near` and `contact` (so no familiarity accrues, and they are never a `Person` target, witness or mood source), ends the activity they were running *and* anyone else's activity that targeted them, and refuses a host-written `Interact` naming them. They keep their slot in `Perception::persons`/`pos`, because scripts and the snapshot index by slot and a shifted list would point every `ctx` call at the wrong person. Removing the marker lets them re-decide on the next tick. `EndActivity { person }` is the sibling for a host-made impossibility (a place closed at night, a shift that ended): the activity ends `completed: false` in the same tick and the person re-decides. `tests/dormant.rs` pins the issue's four criteria plus the exchange refusal and the targeted-activity case.

- **`Locomotion::Host`: the host can own walking, so a game with a street grid, terrain and figures the society does not own can decide *how* a person gets where the society decided they should go.** `SocietySettings::locomotion` is `Builtin` (the default: every person with a `Destination` is walked at `walk_speed`, unchanged and bit-identical) or `Host`. Under `Host` **no crate system writes a person's `Transform`**, `Destination` stays the request to read, and `arrive_and_satisfy` still reads the `Transform` the host moved. `Tuning::travel_timeout_hours` becomes `Option<f32>`: under `Host` only the host can see whether a path exists, so a crate timeout would end activities the host was still walking toward — hence the new `TravelFailed { person }` message, which ends the activity with `ActivityEnded { completed: false }` in the same tick and lets the person re-decide. `None` means a walk never times out. Both startup walk warnings (`warn_short_travel_reach`, `warn_walk_outlasts_activity`) skip under `Host`, since they describe a crate walk that no longer happens. `tests/locomotion.rs` pins the issue's four criteria plus the `Builtin` path. See `docs/loop/needs-decision/travel-time-in-two-clocks.md`, "Decided by the owner, 2026-10-24".

- **`ClockSource::Host`: the host can own the society clock, so a day of unequal-length phases stays in step.** Sanctuary's day is 5 s of dawn for one game hour, 150 s of day for ten, 20 s of dusk for two and 180 s of night for ten; a fixed `seconds_per_day` cannot follow that, so the society's hour drifted from the game's within one day and `Hour` considerations fired at the wrong time. `SocietySettings::clock` is `Fixed` (the default, bit-identical to before) or `Host`, and under `Host` each tick takes the change in a host-written `HostClock { now_days }`. The tick **rate** is untouched — one tick per `tick_secs` of fixed-step time — so a host cannot make the simulation cheaper or dearer by speeding its clock: only how much game time a tick carries changes. A still host clock is a pause (`dt_days = 0`, no need decays, no walk times out), a jump applies once in one tick with no catch-up, a backward clock takes the host's time and carries no negative `dt`, and a `Host` clock with no `HostClock` freezes with one `warn!` rather than inventing time. `tests/clock.rs` pins all of it, including 100 ticks at a still clock decaying no need at all.

### Fixed

- **An activity that targeted a person was cancelled on the tick it began, and it was the single largest source of lost behaviour in the village.** The "the target no longer exists" check in `end_activities` was `perception.persons.binary_search(&target).is_err()`, but `Perception::persons` is filled in `PersonIndex.sorted` order — by `PersonId` — while `binary_search` requires the slice sorted by the key it searches, `Entity`. It is not: printing the raw ids shows them **strictly descending** (`4294967234, 4294967233, …`), because `PersonId` comes from an `On<Add<Person>>` observer whose order need not match spawn order. So the search reported **gone** for people standing right there, and every activity aimed at them ended immediately. On `village-evening` (24 people, 17 game-days, seed 7) that was **1 045 of 1 045** `compliment` interruptions, **47% of every failed activity in the village** — found by adding an `interrupted_by_activity` breakdown to `summary.json`, which also showed that `sleep` was only 162 of them and that a capacity fix (the previous iteration's top item) would have moved nothing. The check is now `!perception.persons.contains(&target)`, matching how every other consumer of this index looks things up. Measured: interrupted endings **2 237 → 400** (22.8% → **5.9%**), `compliment` failures **1 045 → 11**, friends **22 → 31**, rivals **46 → 38**; reverting the one expression restores 2 237 and 1 045 exactly. Total endings fall 9 808 → 6 768 because an activity that died instantly and was re-chosen is now one completed activity — the activity mix keeps its shape (sleep 40%, work 24%, eat 10%, `compliment` now visible at 7%). **The shipped test is a threshold guard, not a reproduction**: six attempts to reproduce the defect all passed under the bug, because it needs the exact `PersonId` ordering the evidence harness produces, and the test's own doc comment says so rather than implying coverage it does not have.

- **A fractional `Hour(5.5)` trigger could never fire, and an integer one could fire twice a day.** `hours_crossed` walked `previous.ceil()` upward in steps of 1.0, so every hour it emitted was a whole number — `Hour(5.5)` was unreachable — and it took its "previous" from `LastHour`, an `f32` hour that is only a whole number for a clock stepping whole hours. At the default `dt_days` the previous hour is `8.002`, whose `ceil` re-crosses hour 9 on every tick inside hour 9. `LastDays(Option<f64>)` now keeps the previous **instant**, one `EventKey::Hours { from, to }` is emitted per tick, and `TriggerKind::Hour(want)` matches when `floor((to*24 - want)/24) > floor((from*24 - want)/24)` — the day count, which increments exactly once as the clock passes `want`. That holds for a fractional `want`, for a span longer than a day, and for a jump across midnight. The baseline is the clock's start instant rather than its first tick, so a trigger standing at the start hour does not fire on the first tick. Documented in `docs/guide/scripting.md` (the event order, rule 2) and `authoring.md`'s trigger table.

- **`dialogue.rs` computed its own `dt` from `seconds_per_day` instead of using the tick's.** Under `ClockSource::Host` that is the wrong number, because the host's clock decides how much game time a tick carries; a conversation's tie and memory effects now scale by the same `clock.dt_days` every other model uses.

- **A `Person`-targeted activity whose target despawned never ended unless a timeout was configured, and its `Destination` never followed the target.** Two separate facts, both found while implementing `Locomotion::Host`. (1) `end_activities` compared the target with `Perception::persons.binary_search`, but that list is ordered by **`PersonId`**, not by `Entity`, so the search was over the wrong ordering and would report a live target gone for any village where the two orders differ; it is now `contains`. (2) The `Destination` rewrite for a moving `Person` target sat *after* the arrival gate that `continue`s when the walker is still far away — which is exactly the case that needs it — so a chase walked to where the target was when the activity started. It now runs before that gate.

### Added

- **A society can be saved and restored, keyed by the host's own ids, and the ticks after a reload are the ticks that would have followed.** Sanctuary autosaves at every dawn and the player can quit and continue; a town whose friendships, grudges, memories and rumours reset on reload is not a society. Nothing in a save is an `Entity` — an entity id is an index into one session's allocator — so every reference to a person is the host-set `PersonKey(u64)` (Sanctuary's roster id) and every place is a `PlaceKey(u64)`, and bevy's `serialize` feature stays off. The new `serde` cargo feature adds `capture(world) -> SocietySave` and `restore(world, save) -> Result<RestoreReport, RestoreError>`: the persons, their personalities, moods, emotions, needs, ties, opinions, knowledge, memories, memberships, status, dormancy and running activity; the clock, the tick accumulator and the RNG's exact word position; every rumor in book order; and the trigger bookkeeping that makes a `once` trigger stay fired and a cooldown stay in force. `restore` **validates everything before writing anything**, so a save from another `VERSION` or one whose definition names have moved returns `Version`/`DefsMismatch` and leaves the world untouched. Measured by `tests/save.rs` on the 24-person village, seed 7: `capture → JSON → restore → capture` is an equal save, and the FNV-1a hash of the society **100 ticks after a restore equals the hash of the uninterrupted run's same 100 ticks** — the same instrument `tests/determinism.rs` uses, now shared as `tests/common/hash.rs`.

- **`Doing` can now say what a person is doing *now*, not what it is walking towards.** `Doing::activity` names the activity that was *chosen*, from the moment it is chosen, while the activity's needs and duration start counting only when `arrived_day` is set — so a host HUD reading it raw shows a village asleep while a third of them are in transit. Measured on the shipped village at 64 s/day, sleep reads **36.8%** of all person-ticks against the **33.3%** one 8-hour sleep a day implies, and the 3.5-point gap is people walking to bed. New `Doing::is_travelling()` and `Doing::label(&SocietyDefs)` answer it: `"travelling:<activity>"` before arrival, the bare name after. `activity` keeps its existing meaning, so **no existing consumer changes behaviour** — `village-evening`'s `series.csv` and `summary.json` are byte-identical before and after. The evidence harness had already invented exactly this convention privately (`format!("travelling:{base}")`), and it now calls `label`, which is the argument for owning it on the component: when two independent readers converge on one workaround, the workaround belongs on the type. Option 2 of `docs/loop/needs-decision/what-doing-reports-while-travelling.md` — a reserved `travelling` activity plus a target field — remains **open** as a schema change a host must approve, and `tests/travel.rs` keeps its `#[ignore]` because it asserts the stronger claim that a walker is being *paid*, which is the separate clock decision. `docs/guide/getting-started.md` now documents the reading in "What is this person doing?".

- **People speak when they have news: contact gossip.** Two people who share a place and are not otherwise exchanging now talk **when the speaker has a rumor the listener does not know**. This is the unshipped half of `how-can-two-groups-ever-meet.md`, whose own recommendation was "a 'people who share a place notice each other' system, worth having for its own sake". The earlier attempt queued a `chat` for every pair in contact and turned a village of 24 into **267 friend pairs** — 97% of every possible pair — because a `chat` is offered whether or not either person has anything to say. The fault was the gate, not the rate: `Knowledge::best_telling` only yields a rumor that is alive, whose subject is not the listener, that the teller still spreads, and that the listener does not know, so a pair with no news between them is offered nothing. On `village-evening` at the same seed the village reads **25 friends / 28 rivals / -0.052 / rumor reach 0.875** against the shipped **22 / 33 / -0.054 / 0.583**: reach up 50%, rivals down 15%, clique failure mode absent. New `Tuning` field `contact_gossip_cooldown_days`, defaulting to **2.0 game-days** — a design default set by measurement, not a paper. The cooldown is load-bearing: every telling to an *informed* listener stifles the teller (Daley & Kendall 1964, DOI 10.1038/2041118a0), so at **0.5 days** the pass burns the spreader pool faster than it spreads news and reach *falls* to **0.375**, while at **6.0 days** it changes nothing at all (0.583). The explicit `theirs.knows(rumor)` check is what keeps a contact-tell from spending a spreader on a neighbour who already knows; omitting it as "redundant" produced the 0.375 regression, which only the reach panel of the evidence plot showed. Civil inattention is the source for acknowledging presence without special interest (Goffman 1963 via Cañigueral & Hamilton 2019, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00560); proximity as the channel is Eckland 1968 via Buss 1993 (DOI 10.1037/0033-295x.100.2.204). `tests/contact_gossip.rs` pins four properties, each verified to fail when its behaviour is removed. `rumor-crosses-a-cluster` **stays `pending`**: the gate opens on news, not distance, so two groups that never share a place never exchange, and the test asserts that so the scenario cannot be marked measured on a number nobody checked.

- **`--friend-affinity` in the evidence harness, and the measurement it made possible: the friendship bar is not the lever — the asymptote is.** Sweeping `Tuning::friend_affinity` from 0.4 to 0.8 moves a 24-person village from **22 to 16** friend-ties, because affinity reaches **1.0** for anyone in contact, so every bar below 1.0 is eventually passed. A run four times longer than any scenario's shows the count is **not a runaway** either: it saturates at **122** friend-ties by game-day 24 (44% of all 276 possible pairs) and decays to 109 by day 57. The problem is a saturating system with a **high asymptote**, and the asymptote is wherever affinity settles. Capping affinity to `[-0.35, 0.35]` — *below* the `0.4` bar, so contact alone cannot make a friend — is the **first shape in six attempts** to bound the network without inverting it: friends **22 -> 15**, rivals **33 -> 18**, holding at three seeds. It costs mood (**-0.054 -> -0.078**), and two different bars give identical mood, which isolates the cost to the clamp: `chat` and `compliment` gate on affinity at `Logistic(8.0, 0.4)` and `Logistic(8.0, 0.7)`, so a lower ceiling means fewer contacts and fewer `Liking` exchanges. One trap is recorded: scaling by the asymptote (`delta · (1 - |a|/K)`) is **asymmetric** — a negative step reaches the outer clamp while a positive one decays — and measured as rivals **rising 33 -> 48**; a plain `clamp(-K, K)` bounds both. The model change was **not** shipped: bounding the network and losing mood is a trade a host should choose, and `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md` now carries every number needed to choose.

- **Six rates that were literals are now on `Tuning`, because the README promised they already were.** `README.md` states that *"every rate is a named field on `Tuning`"*; it was false in two places, both host-facing. `TieLabel::classify` decided friend, rival and stranger from the literals `0.4`, `0.3`, `0.2` and `0.1` — documented *in the same function* as design defaults with no source — and `resolve_interactions` took a literal `0.05` of affinity from a host whose approach was refused. They are now `friend_affinity`, `friend_familiarity`, `rival_affinity`, `rival_familiarity`, `stranger_familiarity` and `rejection_affinity_penalty`, with `classify` taking `&Tuning`. **Defaults equal the literals, so nothing moves:** 67 199 of 67 199 `village-evening` series rows are identical and the village stays at 22 friends / 33 rivals — this is a surface change, and the test proves `classify` reads the fields by raising `friend_affinity` to 0.9 and watching a tie demote from `Friend` to `Acquaintance`. Made visible at a moment when it matters: measured, `friend_familiarity = 0.3` is satisfied by **0.907** among friend-ties and **0.908** among the rest, so that term discriminates nothing at all — a fact only readable in the source until now. See `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md` for why the friendship question itself is still open.

- **A scenario marked `measured` whose observable nothing asserted, and which two documents described differently.** `village-evening`'s observable says a rumor planted at tick 300 is *"told to at least one other person"*; `grep` across `tests/` found nothing asserting any part of it, so four iterations of model change passed through it unverified and every reading had been by eye. - **The positivity offset: an idle person now rests mildly pleased.** The mood model had the *negativity bias* and not the *positivity offset*, and that absence is why pleasure could never be positive. Cacioppo, Cacioppo & Gollan 2013 (DOI `10.1017/S0140525X13002537`) name both: *"the intercept for the positive activation function (i.e., the approach motivation at zero input) is higher than the intercept for the negative activation function (producing the positivity offset), and the gain for the negative activation function is higher than for the positivity activation function (producing the negativity bias)."* The crate had the gain — a landed insult pays Anger 0.5 against a compliment's Joy 0.3, and distress outnumbers joy 3:1 by count — and no intercept, so `Mood::step` returned pleasure to a personality baseline of exactly **zero** for a sampled personality and its ceiling was zero. Measured on `village-evening`, pleasure's maximum over every person and every tick was exactly **+0.000**. `Tuning::positivity_offset` (default 0.1, magnitude a design default) adds the intercept to the resting point: max pleasure **+0.000 → +0.0155** at the default and **+0.0476** at 0.3, mean pleasure **-0.154 → -0.122**, with needs unchanged. Arousal and dominance have no offset and still return to the bare baseline. `positivity_offset: 0.0` reproduces the old behaviour exactly, and a test pins that.

- **`Obstacle`: a way to say a thing is solid.** `steer_movers` passed `&[]` for ORCA's wall list, and `src/steering.rs` said so outright — *"the crate knows no level geometry, so walls are not passed to ORCA; games with walls drive movement themselves."* A host with a tavern therefore had no way to say it was solid, and a person's `Transform` ended inside it: measured on the demo scene, a walker crossed a block whose face was 1.5 m from its centre and came within **0.01 m** of the centre. Attach `Obstacle { radius }` to anything round and solid and it becomes a hard wall half-plane in the solver, with `push_out_of_obstacles` resolving contact — which `bevy_orca` explicitly leaves to the caller. Same walker with an `Obstacle` stops at **2.35 m**. The README clips showed the old behaviour as half-bodies emerging from buildings.

### Changed

- **Both remedies for the friendship clamp's opinion coupling were measured and are dead; the lever is the anchor pull, and lowering it means tuning a cited model.** The previous entry left two options: a longer `ticks:` in the opinion scenario, or a compensating `influence_mu`. Neither works. Under the clamp, opinion spread **asymptotes**: 18 000 and 24 000 ticks both give **0.4036**, so no duration reaches the 0.3405 bar and the scenario can never pass. Doubling `influence_mu` to 0.6 made it **worse** (0.3813 -> 0.4337), because convergence has **two** affinity-scaled paths — Deffuant (`0.5*trust + 0.5*max(0, affinity)`, 0.425 clamped against 0.645 unclamped) and conformity (`max(0.1, affinity + 1)`, **1.35** against **1.79**) — and `mu` scales only the first, so raising it skews the ratio instead of restoring the total. The actual mechanism is that **`anchor_pull` is the only term not affinity-weighted**: `anchor_step = rate * stubbornness * (anchor - x)`. The equilibrium is `social pull / anchor pull`, so clamping halves the numerator and moves every person toward their own anchor — which predicts the stable ~0.40 asymptote exactly. Lowering `anchor_pull` does make the scenario pass, but only at 0.04-0.045, a **2.5x cut** to the Friedkin-Johnsen term, and non-monotonically (0.03 fails again at 0.3720). With all four changes the village reads **16 friends / 10 rivals / -0.052** against the shipped **22 / 33 / -0.054**: rivals down 70%, mood unchanged. It also fails `trust_rises` (accept 0.538 against 0.542) although mean trust rises, because `AcceptDef` centres on trust 0.5. **`affinity` has four readers** — tie labels, Deffuant influence, conformity and acceptance — in three different formulas, three of them invisible from the scenarios the clamp targets. That is why nine attempts failed. Three options are recorded in `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`, with the recommendation to never tune the anchor.

- **The friendship clamp's real cost is the opinion model, and the mood cost turns out to be a curve/gate mismatch that can be repaired.** Last iteration attributed the clamp's cost (`-0.054 -> -0.078`) to fewer affinity-gated contacts. That is true but not the cause: `compliment` gates on `Affinity` at `Logistic(8.0, 0.7)`, which scores **0.057** at the clamp ceiling of `0.35` against **0.673** at the unclamped mean of `0.79` — compliments are **12x less likely**, purely from where the gate sits relative to the ceiling. Moving the gate to `Logistic(16.0, 0.25)` recovers mood to **-0.045**, better than the `-0.054` baseline. The friend bar must also move under the ceiling (`0.4` is unreachable at `0.35`), so with `friend_affinity` at `0.30` and a trust second input the village reads **16 friends / 10 rivals / -0.045** against the shipped **22 / 33 / -0.054** — better on all three axes. **It still cannot ship:** `tests/opinion_convergence.rs` fails, because Deffuant influence is **weighted by tie strength**, so halving the affinity ceiling halves how fast opinions converge — spread falls **25%** where the scenario asserts **33%**. `affinity` is therefore shared between liking *and* influence, and a scenario's tick count is silently a statement about that rate. None of the six earlier attempts found this, because none changed the affinity *scale*; they changed rates, gates or the accumulation shape. Recorded with three options in `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`.

- **Five mechanisms have now been tested against "everyone becomes friends", and none works — with one cause.** The thresholds in `TieLabel::classify` were the previous iteration's hypothesis. Measured, they do not discriminate: friend-ties average affinity **0.792** against **-0.223** for the rest, but familiarity is **0.907 against 0.908** — no separation at all — while trust separates sevenfold (**0.480 against 0.068**). Gating `Friend` on trust therefore looked right and did nothing: at `trust >= 0.6` the village is unchanged at **22 friends / 33 rivals**, and with a contact-triggered exchange at 3 h it is **267 / 0**, because `chat` pays `trust: 0.05` per accepted exchange so contact talk adds **+0.4 trust per day** and trust saturates exactly as affinity does. Saturating all three accumulators (affinity, trust, respect) gives 23 / 43 alone and **268 / 0** with contact, because saturation bounds at **1.0** and every threshold is at or below **0.4**. The finding that covers all five failures: **no tie variable can sit below a threshold for a pair that has had contact** — all four accumulate, every exchange supplies all of them, so any *level*-based gate is passed by contact volume. Reverted; the crate is unchanged. The only untested shape is an asymptote **below** the threshold, recorded in `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`.

- **The recommended fix for "everyone becomes friends" was tested and does not work.** `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md` recommended saturating interaction affinity — `affinity + gain · (1 − |affinity|)`, the form exposure already uses — on the reasoning that the *n*-th conversation should be worth less than the first. Built and measured on `village-evening`: **saturation alone** is nearly neutral (friends **22 → 22**, rivals 33 → 38), but it does not survive the mechanism that caused the problem — with a contact-triggered exchange at 3 h it gives **260 friends and 0 rivals**, against **267 and 0** without it. Both curves cross the `0.4` Friend threshold, and both cross it within 45 exchanges, so a saturating gain changes **when** friendship happens and not **whether**. Reverted; the crate is unchanged at friends 22 / rivals 33. The recommendation is retracted, and the decision file now ranks the untested shapes instead — recommending a **gate on trust** next, because trust already exists in the crate and moves far more slowly than affinity, which is the only property that can bound a friendship that contact alone would otherwise create. No paper in the corpus supplies the saturating shape: the closest, Reiter et al. 2023 (DOI `10.31234/osf.io/34vnx`), is about developmental trajectories of trust, not per-exchange diminishing returns. This is the **third** mechanism to fail on one problem, after a `help` activity (rivals 44 → 0) and a contact exchange (friends 22 → 267).

- **A contact-triggered exchange was built, measured and reverted: it turns a village into a clique.** `resolve_interactions` fires an exchange when a person *arrives* at an activity, once, so two people who arrive hours apart share a place and not a moment — measured, two co-located people produce **30** exchanges in 20 000 ticks and `rumor-crosses-a-cluster`'s two groups share a market for **434** ticks and exchange **nothing**. A `talk_in_contact` system walking the `Perception.contact` neighbourhood that `expose_contacts` already computes fixed that (**30 -> 89**, cooldown verified tick by tick at 1 920 ticks for 3 game-hours) — and **broke the village**: friends **22 -> 267** and rivals **33 -> 0** at a 3-hour cooldown, 182/3 at 12 hours and 72/12 at 48. **267 friend pairs in a village of 24 is 97% of all possible pairs**, because the contact pass adds **13 742** requests over 17 game-days against roughly 7 500 baseline interactions: in a village everyone is always in contact, so any rate high enough to bridge a 40 m gap is high enough to make everyone friends. The mechanism conflates *meeting* with *befriending* — two people sharing a tavern are aware of each other, they do not become friends by standing. Reverted; the crate is at friends 22 / rivals 33. The measurement and three options are in `docs/loop/needs-decision/how-can-two-groups-ever-meet.md`. This is the second mechanism to produce a clique from a plausible local rule — a `help` activity did the same two iterations earlier (rivals 44 -> 0) — so the village's tie formation is far more sensitive to contact volume than its rates suggest.

- **`rumor-crosses-a-cluster` fails, and the cause is arrival timing rather than distance.** The scenario's observable says a rumor crosses to 40% of the second group when one person from each is *"brought together once"*, and it has been `pending` since November with nothing checking it. Measured, the rumor reaches **0 of 6** at 64 s/day and **0 of 6** at 240, over the same game-days. Six layers were checked separately and **all work** — steering walks at 1.4 m/s, **359 948** person-ticks of movement, a bridge place chosen for **36 489**, **6 720** ticks arrived, **434 ticks** with both groups arrived, and one person seeing two of the other group at **2.89 m** — while cross-group interactions are **0**. The cause: **an exchange fires on *arrival*, once.** Two people placed together in the smallest case give **39 interactions** over 20 000 ticks, so the mechanism is sound, but a pair arriving at tick 100 and tick 500 shares 400 ticks of standing together and shares no arrival — and they arrive hours apart by construction, because a 20 m walk is **5.4 game-hours** against a **0.75-hour** activity. This is `travel-time-in-two-clocks.md` producing an observable outcome for the first time. `tests/cluster_reach.rs` measures the second group of six (not `rumor_reach`, which divides by all twelve) and **asserts the current failure**, so marking the scenario `measured` requires deleting that line deliberately. Two fixes were tried and measured before the cause was found: a crossroads carrying `gathering` left the reach at 0.167 because `Place` targets pick the *nearest* match and each group's own hearth is 2 m away; a market carrying a tag the hearths lack was chosen for 36 489 ticks and still reached 0 of 6.

- **`need-cycle` asked a need to starve that the rates make impossible, so it had been `pending` since November.** Its observable said *"`rest` and `food` fall and rise again over a game-day: each crosses below 0.35 and recovers above 0.7"*. Measured per person at the scenario's own settings (64 s/day, 80 000 ticks), `rest` never crosses 0.35 at **any** seed and cannot: `sleep` restores `0.15/game-hour x 8 h = 1.20` against a `rest` decay of `0.70/day`, a net **+0.50 per sleep**, so it ratchets up until sleeping stops being chosen and never needs to run low. `food` cycles **8 of 8 people** at both seeds because eating supplies `1.2 x 0.75 h = 0.90` against an exactly equal `0.9/day` decay, and `fun` cycles **8 and 6 of 8** — which the observable's own correction (*"only `food` cycles"*) denied, and which the test printed without asserting while its name said `only_food_cycles`. The observable is restated to what the model does — every need is met and re-met, none left falling with no activity to satisfy it — the per-seed cycle counts are recorded rather than asserted as constants, the test asserts the restated property, and the scenario is `measured` for the first time. The rates were **not** retuned to fit the old claim: the decay is cited (Maslow 1943) and the rates are data a host sets.

- **A deed seen by a crowd now costs each witness less.** `witness_reaction` applied `trust_gain * harm` at full magnitude to **every** witness within perception, so a deed's total cost grew linearly with the crowd — while a trust *gain* from an interaction could only ever reach the two parties. One `chat` was `+0.10` in total against an insult seen by three people at `-0.42`, and `village-evening`'s mean trust sat at 0.345 against the 0.5 that `AcceptDef` treats as neutral. `Tuning::witness_dilution` (default 0.5) divides the cost by `witnesses ^ dilution`: measured, one witnessed insult costs each witness **0.0800** with one bystander and **0.0431** with five, where before it was 0.0800 in both. Trust at an exchange **0.345 -> 0.435**, accept rate **0.533 -> 0.558**; `village-evening` mean pleasure **-0.127 -> -0.046** at seed 42 and **-0.110 -> -0.052** at seed 99, with rivals **50 -> 34** at seed 42, while seed 7 barely moved (-0.058 -> -0.054) and seed 99's rivals rose 45 -> 47 — recorded rather than averaged away. The dilution touches the deed's *magnitude* only: affinity, respect and the emotion keep their sign and full weight. Sommerfeld, Krambeck, Semmann & Milinski 2007 (DOI `10.1073/pnas.0704598104`) measured the mechanism directly: gossip transmission has *"a dampening effect ... the more extreme a gossip was, the less impact it had on the reader"*, and believability rises with the number of sources. `witness_dilution: 0.0` restores the old behaviour.

- **A pleasant conversation now registers emotionally, for both people.** `chat` and `gossip` paid `social` to both parties and **no emotion to either**, so 43% of accepted interactions in the starter pack — the two most common — left no mark on anyone's mood, and the actor who initiated a pleasant exchange gained no affect from it in any interaction (`compliment` paid only its target). Measured over 20 000 ticks and 24 people: **1 216 refusals produced exactly 1 216 distress appraisals**, while **1 338 acceptances produced 443 positive ones**. Both interactions now pay mutual `Liking` on accept (0.2 and 0.15, design defaults): positive appraisals **443 → 1 491**, the negative:positive ratio **3.58:1 → 1.06:1**, and mean pleasure **-0.122 → -0.066**, holding at three seeds (-0.127 → -0.065 at 42, -0.110 → -0.070 at 99) with needs unchanged. At the end of a run the steady state went from Anger 14 / Distress 4 / **Joy 0 / Liking 0** to Anger 14 / Distress 25 / **Joy 22 / Liking 14**, and max pleasure from -0.047 to **+0.237**: positive affect was absent and is now present. The negativity **bias** is deliberate and unchanged — Cacioppo, Cacioppo & Gollan 2013 (DOI `10.1017/S0140525X13002537`) give the negative channel the steeper gain, so an insult still pays `Anger 0.5` against a conversation's `Liking 0.2`.

- **A refused interaction's distress is now a named `Tuning` field, and a definition's own `on_reject` replaces it.** The refusal emotion was a literal `0.3` in `exchange` — the crate's only unnamed emotion intensity, against its own rule that every rate is a named field. `Tuning::rejection_distress` names it (design default, OCC mechanism cited). More importantly, **two paths for one event** are gone: a pack's `on_reject` emotion now *replaces* the crate's rather than stacking with it. Measured at identical accept/refuse counts, mean pleasure over eight people: literal 0.3 with no `on_reject` in the pack **-0.202**; pack `on_reject 0.1` with both emitted **-0.207** — worse than doing nothing; pack `on_reject 0.1` replacing the fallback **-0.165**. The starter pack's `chat` and `gossip` gain `on_reject: (actor_emotion: Some((Distress, 0.1)))`, so a refused greeting is mildly deflating rather than a small tragedy.

- **Perception runs on a uniform grid, and the crate's own account of its scale curve was wrong.** `perceive` scanned every pair, and `docs/guide/concepts.md`, `examples/benchmark.rs` and `scenarios.yaml` all stated that this quadratic search was what a host pays for. It is now a uniform grid with one cell per `perception_radius` -- the direct reading of Ericson 2005, *Real-Time Collision Detection*, §7.1.1 for points -- filled in ascending index order so a person's neighbours come out **exactly** as the all-pairs scan produced them: 67 199 of 67 199 `village-evening` series rows are identical, and `tests/perception.rs` pins the identity across five populations and two radii. Measured: **254.5 -> 245.1 ms** a tick at 5 000 people, **14.6 -> 13.5 ms** at 1 000. **3-7%, not a rescaling** -- because the instrumented breakdown shows `perceive` is **36 ms of a ~320 ms fixed step** at 5 000 people. What actually scales is **scoring**: every `Person`-targeted activity is scored against every neighbour, and at 5 000 people on the benchmark's 40 m disc an 8 m radius genuinely holds **182** neighbours (0.995 people per m²), so a tick runs roughly `5 000 x 4 x 182 = 3.6M` consideration evaluations against 144 000 at 1 000. The docs now say that, and say that no index removes it.

- **Four of the guide's ten trigger forms did not parse, so a host copying them got a byte offset and no explanation.** `docs/guide/authoring.md` printed `RumorReach { deed, fraction }`, `NeedBelow { need, below }` and `DialogueNode { conversation, node }` in **brace** form for struct variants — RON writes those with parentheses, so each fails with `Expected opening (` — and printed `NormViolated(deed)` with a field name for a **tuple** variant, which fails with `Expected string`. Two traps are stated where they bite: struct variants take parentheses, and `TieLabel(Friend)` in RON is `"friend"` in a Rhai condition, both correct and twenty lines apart. The opening definitions file and the conversation block are covered by the same test, and those passed unchanged.

- **`mere_exposure_per_day` cited Zajonc 1968 as though the paper supplied the rate.** Zajonc (DOI `10.1037/h0025848`) supplies the *mechanism* — repeated exposure increases liking — and no number, so `0.05` is a design default. The doc now says so, and states what neither rate reveals alone: `mere_exposure_per_day` and `affinity_relax_per_day` are two first-order processes on one variable, so affinity settles at `0.05 / (0.05 + 0.01) = 0.833` — **52% above the `0.4` that `TieLabel::classify` calls a Friend**, which makes proximity *sufficient* for friendship. Three sweeps are recorded there because they **disproved the hypothesis that this was the cause**: lowering exposure to 0.02, 0.01 and 0.005 moves the village's friends only **22 → 15 → 15 → 16**, and lowering `balance_rate_per_day` from 0.5 to 0.05 moves it **22 → 25**. What does drive it is the **number of exchanges**: measured, a pair becomes a Friend after **45** interactions with affinity still climbing to 0.73 by 57, because familiarity from exposure saturates (`1 − f`) while affinity from interaction does not. Friendship is therefore a monotone function of contact volume — friend-ties grow **25 → 115** across 17 game-days without flattening — which is the single cause behind the two mechanisms reverted in the two previous iterations (a `help` activity drove rivals 44 → 0; a contact-triggered exchange drove friends 22 → 267). `tests/exposure_equilibrium.rs` pins the equilibrium arithmetic; the model change that would fix it is `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`.

- **Nothing in the starter pack could raise trust, so every acceptance draw subtracted it.** `AcceptDef` scores an interaction as `base + affinity·a + trust·(t - 0.5) + ...`, so trust is centred on 0.5 — and `Tie::new` starts every stranger there. `TieDelta` carries a `trust` field that any interaction can set, and **the word `trust` did not occur anywhere in `assets/starter.society.ron`**: the only thing that moved it was `witness_reaction`'s `trust_gain * harm`, which is negative for every deed the pack actually uses (`help` and `gift` were defined and never used; only `insult` carried a deed). Measured with 24 people, the trust a target held toward the person approaching it was **0.13-0.27** depending on the interaction — every one below neutral — and **47% of interactions were refused**. `chat`, `gossip` and `compliment` now raise trust on accept (0.05, 0.03, 0.02). Measured as a controlled comparison, the same pack with the deltas stripped against the shipped one: mean trust **0.261 -> 0.345**, accept rate **0.486 -> 0.533**; `village-evening` mean pleasure **-0.066 -> -0.058**, positive person-ticks **3.4% -> 5.2%**, friends/rivals **21/44 -> 24/35**, needs unchanged. Trust still declines over a long run, and the reason is structural and now a decision: a trust *gain* reaches two parties while a trust *loss* reaches **every witness** at full magnitude, so one chat is `+0.10` and one insult seen by three people is `-0.42` — the penalty multiplies with the crowd and the reward cannot. - **A grudge was the count of offences, not the worst one — and half a villager's evening went to snubbing.** `Memories::grudge` summed `max(0, -valence) * recency` over every memory about a person and clamped at 1. What remains is the curve: `Linear(-1.0, 1.0)` scores a tavern 6.5 m away inside an 8 m radius at **0.19**, which is too harsh for a destination walked to once a day, and that shape is `needs-decision/travel-cost-is-not-scored.md`'s question.

Also new: `SocietyDefs::sources_for(need)`, so a host can see every activity that supplies a need — `best_source` alone hid the fact that `fun` had exactly one.

- **A need whose sources cannot meet its decay is now named, with the arithmetic.** Every need decays per game-day and every activity supplies `satisfies · duration_hours` per use; the starter pack's budget is `social` 2.78 uses/day, `rest` 0.58, `food` 1.00 and `fun` **1.33**. `fun` gets 0.10, so it sits at zero for a third of a run. New `SocietyDefs::need_decay` and `best_source` expose the arithmetic, and `BehaviorPlugin::finish` warns when a source needs more than four uses a day. **Sizing `relax` to fix `fun` was tried and reverted:** `satisfies 0.4 → 0.55` halves `fun`'s zero fraction (33.8% → 18.8%) but lowers the mean of all four needs (0.127 → **0.118**), because the village is over-subscribed and one supply only crowds out another. The open question is `docs/loop/needs-decision/over-subscribed-evening.md`.

- **One GIF per feature, and three defects the clips exposed.** `examples/capture` takes `--scene village|mood|opinions|needs|ties|factions`, each a different view of the **same run** (identical `rumor_reach 0.880, friends 36, rivals 45, barks 2810`), and `docs/media/` now holds six clips. Looking at the frames found: every overlay drew at 6 cm and was hidden by the capsules and the ground plane's depth (the ties clip showed two segments where the graph has hundreds of edges); `draw_rumor_rings`, `stats()` and the HUD all read `RumorId(0)`, so the hero clip reported **0.000 reach** for a rumor that reached **0.880** of the village; and the mood and opinion colour scales mapped the model's actual ±0.4 range onto ±1, so every villager looked identical. Scales are now normalised to the measured range (`MOOD_SPAN = 0.45`).

- **A timeout that cannot reach the places is now named at startup.** `Tuning::travel_timeout_hours` is game hours while `SocialMover::speed` is metres per real second, and nothing told a host they must agree. `BehaviorPlugin::finish` now computes how much walking a timeout buys and warns when it is under 10 m. Measured: a 40-hour timeout at `seconds_per_day = 4` buys **9.3 m**, and the project's own village needed 13 m — a 13 m walk takes **527 ticks** against the 427 allowed, so far-corner villagers always timed out and **78 of 108** `eat`s were interrupted. The evidence harness's timeout is corrected from 40 to 55 game-hours, which lifts `fun`'s starvation from 55% of ticks to 35% without fixing the rest.
- **`need-cycle`'s blocker note was wrong and is corrected.** It claimed a back-to-back sleep loop "with the shipped rates". The starter pack's `sleep` is gated `Hours(22, 6)` and ranked 1, so one person sleeps **10 times in 10.1 days**; the loop came from a test-local definition with no hour gate. Re-measured, `food` and `fun` both cycle [0.000, 1.000] and cross the scenario's thresholds, while `social` and `rest` never fall below 0.364 and 0.495. The old note stays in the file under `superseded:`.

- **`DecisionTrace`: why a person did that.** A bounded, opt-in record of every decision — each option that scored, its score, and the one a weighted draw took. `Doing` says what a person is doing; nothing said what else it weighed or by how much. Built to the three constraints Game AI Pro 3 ch. 6 puts on a recorder: **no impact on the outcome** (asserted — the same seed gives a byte-identical sequence of choices with and without the trace), **fixed memory** (a ring of `capacity` decisions, oldest dropped), and **no measurable cost** (15.16 ms/tick with it against 15.28 without, at 1 000 people). `Decision::describe` renders one line: `tick 4147: someone chose compliment (0.752), 25% below snub (1.000)`.

### Fixed

- **The scenarios now measure what they claim.** Three of the four `pending` scenarios in `docs/loop/scenarios.yaml` are `measured`, each with a test named in the file: `deed-draws-a-response` (`tests/deed_response.rs`), `opinion-converges-but-not-entirely` (`tests/opinion_convergence.rs`) and `determinism` (whose tests had been green for several iterations). `need-cycle` stays `pending` with a measured blocker: a person sleeps back to back — 29 four-hour sleeps in 20 000 ticks, and four game-hours *is* 683 ticks — so `rest` stays at 1.000 and never decays.
- **`examples/evidence.rs`'s `summary.json` now follows the planted rumor.** It counted knowers of `RumorId(0)` — the first rumor the run made — so every scenario that plants a later one reported `rumor_known_by: 0`, including `need-cycle`, which printed 0 knowers beside 119 rumors. `village-evening` now reports 1 (matching its 0.042 reach) and `rumor-crosses-a-cluster` 4 (matching 0.333).

- **A person's rumor memory is now bounded.** `Knowledge` had no cap while `Memories` (64) and `Ties` (150, Dunbar) both did, so it grew to **344 entries per person after 4 000 ticks** on 12 people. New `SocietySettings::knowledge_capacity`, default 64, evicts the entry with the lowest `belief · 0.995^(hours since heard)` — the crate's own telling weight minus the valence `Knowledge` cannot see. Behaviour at the defaults is unchanged: all 4 200 series rows and the whole narrative of `village-evening` are byte-identical. Worth 2.3% of a long run's tick cost.
- **The evidence harness now follows the rumor it plants.** `series.csv`'s `rumor_reach` read `RumorId(0)` — the first rumor the run ever made, unrelated to the planted one — so three iterations recorded "rumor reach flat-lines" from a column that was never measuring the scenario. With the planted id, `rumor-crosses-a-cluster` shows a real diffusion curve (0 → 0.333 over ~350 ticks) instead of one step, and `village-evening` shows **0.042**, its origin having gossiped twice in 3 600 ticks.

- **An activity's odds no longer scale with how many targets it has.** `targets_for` gives a `Person` activity one candidate per person in perception and a `Place` activity one per matching place, and selection drew over every candidate — so an activity's selection mass was `weight · score · (number of targets)`. In the starter pack that gave `gossip` and `snub` (which satisfy **no need**) 80% of all decisions, while `eat` and `relax` took 2.7% each, and need starvation grew with the number of neighbours: `fun` at 0 for 5.9% of ticks with one person, **43.4%** with sixteen. Dill's weight-based draw is over *options*, and an option is an action, so scoring now keeps each activity's best target (and its ties) as one candidate; the target is then picked uniformly from the ties from the seeded RNG. Measured: a `Person` activity's share of starts went from **0.97 to 0.50** at twelve neighbours, `fun` starvation from 43.4% to **7.7%**, and `food` from 39.3% to **1.9%** with travel removed.

- **`ActivityStarted` and `ActivityEnded` are now a balanced pair.** A start is written when a person **decides**, which is the instant its `Doing` is inserted and `Doing::started_day` is stamped — not when it arrives. Before, a start was written only on arrival, while an end was written for *any* removed `Doing`, so a person whose target was out of reach emitted ends for activities no host was ever told about: **2 starts against 55 ends** for one person 50 m from its place, and 2 667 unpaired ends at 40 people. Arrival keeps its own meaning (`Doing::arrived_day` begins the duration and the `satisfies` payout). A host will notice that activity barks now fire as a person sets off rather than on arrival, which is the better reading of a line like "Back to it.".

- **The quick start now works, and is compiled.** Two documents that tell a host how to begin were wrong: `README.md`'s quick start took **0 society ticks in 600 updates** (a headless app has no frame clock, so `Time<Fixed>` never advances without `TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration`), and `docs/guide/getting-started.md`'s first block panicked in `DialoguePlugin::finish` without a speech-bubbles camera. Both fixed. The quick start is now a **running doctest** in `src/lib.rs` — `cargo test --doc` executes it and asserts `tick > 0` — and `tests/readme.rs` asserts the README's block is byte-equal to it, so they cannot drift. That doctest immediately caught a hand-written repair naming a `TimeUpdateStrategy` that Bevy 0.20 does not re-export in its prelude.

### Added

- **Startup warnings are now asserted, not read by hand.** `tests/common/capture.rs` is a `LogPlugin` layer that records every event into a resource, so a test can assert a `warn!`. Building it immediately found a defect in the previous iteration's warning: an activity with both a `Field` consideration and a `Gradient` target was counted twice, so one offending definition was reported as `2 definitions … — follow, follow`. Now one. Note the constraint: the `tracing` subscriber is process-global and settable once, so a test binary may build only one capturing app.

- **A startup warning for definitions that rely on unscored field inputs.** `Signal::Field` considerations and `Gradient` targets are accepted by the definitions file with the `fields` feature on, and the decision scores neither — the field grids and `SocialFields::field_value` work, but nothing reads them when choosing. `BehaviorPlugin` now warns and names the offending activities, and `SocietyDefs::{activity_signals,has_gradient_activity}` plus a public `Signal` let a host run the same check. Why the wiring could not be completed this iteration is written up in `docs/loop/needs-decision/field-inputs-are-unscored.md`.

- **A cost harness, and the numbers behind the scale claim.** `examples/benchmark` reports milliseconds per society tick for a given population. Measured on an M1 Max, release, starter pack: 0.55 ms per tick at 100 persons, **14.1 ms at 1 000** (4.9% of a 60 fps frame at `tick_secs = 0.25`), and **351.6 ms at 5 000** (140% of a frame — unplayable). Reproducible to 0.01%, and quadratic exactly as `docs/guide/concepts.md` claimed: 25× the pairs costs 25.0× the time. The guide's Scale section now carries the table instead of the word "fine".

- **`ctx.tellable(a)`**, and a starter `gossip` that uses it. `ctx.knows` needs a rumor id, which an authored definitions file has no way to name, so no script could ask "does this person have anything to tell?". `ctx.tellable(a)` answers whether a person knows a rumor they are still spreading, and the starter pack's `gossip` now scores 0 without one — gossip carries reputation information (Sommerfeld et al. 2007), so a villager with none converses instead. Measured in the village: `chat` 8746 → 9312 person-ticks, `gossip` down 6%, and `snub` down **73%** (6004 → 1617), because fewer gratuitous gossips means fewer insults and fewer grudges.

- **A startup warning for degenerate activity durations.** `ActivitySpec::duration_hours` is game hours while a tick is `dt_days`, so a short `seconds_per_day` can make an activity last a single tick — at which point a person re-decides every tick and repeats whatever scored highest. That was silent, and cost three iterations of findings. `BehaviorPlugin` now warns with the activity's own arithmetic (`snub (0.1h = 1.1 ticks)`), and `SocietyDefs::activity_durations()` lets a host make the same check.

- **The hostility runaway was three things, and none was missing damping.** `snub`'s grudge curve saturated at grudge 0.4, so one witnessed insult made snubbing the best option forever: `Linear(2.5, 0)` becomes `Linear(1.0, 0)`. The cluster scenario gave its people no places, so gossip held the social need high enough that `chat`'s own `Need(social) Linear(-1, 1)` gate never opened — measured, `chat` ran zero times in 2000 ticks — and each group now has a hearth. And the evidence harness ran a 60× shorter game-day than the definitions were calibrated for, where a 0.1-hour `snub` lasted 1.07 ticks and people re-decided every tick. See `docs/research/rumor-and-reputation.md` (Takács, Flache & Mäs 2016, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0157948, for why no damping rule was added).

- **Rivalry, and three defects that stood in its way.** `snub` was gated on *low* affinity, so a village snubbed strangers and never anyone it knew; it now reads `ctx.grudge(actor, target)`. Script considerations could not see their target at all (`target` was bound to −1), so no `ctx` call could score a `Person` option. And `witness_reaction` signed approval by the witness's opinion of the *norm* rather than of the *act*, so a kindness-valuing witness rewarded an insult: it now uses `norm · sign(harm)`. Measured, `rumor-crosses-a-cluster` goes from 11 rivals to 30 and `need-cycle` from 0 to 21. See `docs/research/rumor-and-reputation.md` (Bondü & Richter 2016, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00795, for the mechanism).

- **`chat` and `gossip` satisfy `social` while they run.** They were the two activities that serve the social need and the only two that declared no `satisfies` rate, so time spent talking counted for nothing while `eat` (+1.2/h) and `sleep` (+0.15/h) both paid out. Measured over 4200 ticks at the same seed, mean `social` rose from 0.105 to 0.225.

- **Thought barks on activity start.** An activity's `barks` lines are written as `SocietyBark { thought: true }` when it starts, with `bark_chance`, through a system that reads `ActivityStarted` so the draw happens once per start in `PersonIndex` order. The dead-code inventory is now empty: every public item the crate declares is reachable.

- **Movement and gossip reach the capture.** `Destination` was set only for a `Point` target, so a village whose activities all target places never walked: everybody stood where they spawned. Every target now gets one — a place's transform, or a person's position offset by `0.75 × interaction_radius` — and `tests/behavior.rs` pins both. The starter pack's `gossip` activity is gated on distance rather than familiarity, so a rumor can spread in one evening instead of waiting for ties, and the capture counts barks across its settle steps rather than only the recorded frames.

- **Daily life and social exchanges (`BehaviorPlugin`).** Autonomous people choose activities by compensated utility over needs, mood, personality, emotions, ties and the clock; they travel to a point, place, home or person, arrive within `interaction_radius`, satisfy needs at the definition's per-hour rate, and end an activity on duration, on a lost person target, or on a travel timeout. A social exchange runs the `requires` script, the acceptance draw, both effect sets, and on acceptance opinion influence, gossip, a contest, a judged deed, triadic closure and intergroup contact; both sides record an `Interaction` memory and gain exposure. `InteractionResolved`, `ActivityStarted` and `ActivityEnded` report it, and the trigger queue sees exchanges and activities in the same tick. See `docs/research/needs-and-utility.md` (Mark & Dill, Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13; Dill 2015, DOI 10.1201/b18373-5).

- **Examples scaffolding.** `examples/common/recorder.rs` is the headless recorder the capture example drives: no window, no winit, synchronous pipeline compilation, an off-screen render target, a hand-pumped loop with a device poll per frame, and `TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration(1/30 s)` so a captured frame is exactly one thirtieth of a second of game time. `tools/gif.sh` is the other half: a two-pass palette encode with the caption burned in.

- **Script runtime and rules (`RulesPlugin`).** The `ctx` / `fx` Rhai API over a per-tick snapshot: `ctx` reads the society as it stood at the tick start and `fx` queues writes that one exclusive system applies after `SocietySystems::Rules`. Triggers fire on `Tick`, `Hour`, `Custom` (`FireTrigger`), `TieLabelChanged`, `NormViolated`, `NeedBelow` and `RumorReach`, honouring `once` and `cooldown_hours`; a failed condition counts as false and a failed effect applies none of its actions. Every evaluation is sandboxed (10 000 operations, bounded strings, arrays and maps).

- **Dialogue (`DialoguePlugin`, feature `dialogue`).** Conversations and barks through `bevy_speech_bubbles` at its pinned rev. `TalkTo { npc, player }` opens the highest-priority conversation rule whose `if` holds against the tick-start snapshot, and is ignored while a conversation is open. Node effects run their script; each finished conversation records a `Talked` memory for both people and a two-way exposure. `fx.converse` queues a `TalkTo`. `SocietyBark` lines are forwarded under the speaker key `society:<PersonId>`, which lives as long as the person. The plugin runs in `Update` because speech bubbles are frame-driven.

- **Beliefs, rumors and reputation (`BeliefsPlugin`).** Opinions per topic with bounded-confidence influence, Friedkin–Johnsen anchor pull and conformity to a strong group mean; a rumor book with the Daley–Kendall stifling rule and trust-scaled belief; and the witness, patient and actor reactions to a deed, including `NormViolated`. `PlantRumor` starts a rumor from the game or a script. See `docs/research/opinion-dynamics.md` and `docs/research/rumor-and-reputation.md` (Deffuant et al. 2000, DOI 10.1142/S0219525900000078; Daley & Kendall 1964, DOI 10.1038/2041118a0; Ohtsuki & Iwasa 2004, DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2004.06.005).

- **Engine-free model math (`bevy_society::model`).** Response curves, the Mehrabian/ALMA personality-to-mood baseline, power-law familiarity and exposure, Deffuant opinion influence with anchor pull and conformity, Daley–Kendall stifling, and triadic balance. Each formula's DOI sits in a comment beside it.

- **Relationships (`RelationshipsPlugin`).** Directed, weighted ties per person with power-law familiarity (Anderson & Schooler 1991), mere exposure (Zajonc 1968), affinity relaxation toward zero, Heider structural balance over sampled triads, a Dunbar-style cap on ties, and labels (stranger, acquaintance, friend, rival) with a `TieLabelChanged` message. See `docs/research/relationships.md` (Heider 1946, DOI 10.1080/00223980.1946.9917275; Dunbar 1992, DOI 10.1016/0047-2484(92)90081-J).

- **Groups and status (`GroupsPlugin`).** Faction memberships with an in-group bias, standing as respect conferred by familiar persons, logistic contests weighted by dominance and standing, intergroup contact (Pettigrew & Tropp 2006), and per-faction cohesion and regard each tick. See `docs/research/groups-and-status.md` (Tajfel et al. 1971, DOI 10.1002/ejsp.2420010202; Bonabeau et al. 1995, DOI 10.1016/0378-4371(95)00064-e).

- **Social fields (`fields` feature).** Per-cell crowd presence and commotion grids plus one presence grid per faction, deposited in person order and evaporated per game-day on `bevy_stigmergy`. Schelling's tolerance input is the `OwnShare` field (own / (own + other)), and `Gradient` activity targets follow the field toward or away from a cluster. See `docs/research/groups-and-status.md` (Schelling 1971, DOI 10.1080/0022250X.1971.9989794).

- **Mind (`MindPlugin`).** Emotions (OCC kinds with ALMA PAD values) are appraised by `Appraise` messages and decay with a 0.1 game-day half-life. Mood is pulled toward the intensity-weighted emotion centre and returns to a personality baseline (Mehrabian 1996; Gebhard 2005), with Hatfield-style contagion from affinity-weighted contacts. Needs decay at `decay_per_day`. See `docs/research/personality-and-mood.md` (DOI 10.1080/00049539608259510).
