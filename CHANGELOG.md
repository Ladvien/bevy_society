# Changelog

All notable changes to bevy_society. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); the crate is pre-1.0, so any release may break the API.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **`ClockSource::Host`: the host can own the society clock, so a day of unequal-length phases stays in step.** Sanctuary's day is 5 s of dawn for one game hour, 150 s of day for ten, 20 s of dusk for two and 180 s of night for ten; a fixed `seconds_per_day` cannot follow that, so the society's hour drifted from the game's within one day and `Hour` considerations fired at the wrong time. `SocietySettings::clock` is `Fixed` (the default, bit-identical to before) or `Host`, and under `Host` each tick takes the change in a host-written `HostClock { now_days }`. The tick **rate** is untouched — one tick per `tick_secs` of fixed-step time — so a host cannot make the simulation cheaper or dearer by speeding its clock: only how much game time a tick carries changes. A still host clock is a pause (`dt_days = 0`, no need decays, no walk times out), a jump applies once in one tick with no catch-up, a backward clock takes the host's time and carries no negative `dt`, and a `Host` clock with no `HostClock` freezes with one `warn!` rather than inventing time. `tests/clock.rs` pins all of it, including 100 ticks at a still clock decaying no need at all.

### Fixed

- **A fractional `Hour(5.5)` trigger could never fire, and an integer one could fire twice a day.** `hours_crossed` walked `previous.ceil()` upward in steps of 1.0, so every hour it emitted was a whole number — `Hour(5.5)` was unreachable — and it took its "previous" from `LastHour`, an `f32` hour that is only a whole number for a clock stepping whole hours. At the default `dt_days` the previous hour is `8.002`, whose `ceil` re-crosses hour 9 on every tick inside hour 9. `LastDays(Option<f64>)` now keeps the previous **instant**, one `EventKey::Hours { from, to }` is emitted per tick, and `TriggerKind::Hour(want)` matches when `floor((to*24 - want)/24) > floor((from*24 - want)/24)` — the day count, which increments exactly once as the clock passes `want`. That holds for a fractional `want`, for a span longer than a day, and for a jump across midnight. The baseline is the clock's start instant rather than its first tick, so a trigger standing at the start hour does not fire on the first tick. Documented in `docs/guide/scripting.md` (the event order, rule 2) and `authoring.md`'s trigger table.

- **`dialogue.rs` computed its own `dt` from `seconds_per_day` instead of using the tick's.** Under `ClockSource::Host` that is the wrong number, because the host's clock decides how much game time a tick carries; a conversation's tie and memory effects now scale by the same `clock.dt_days` every other model uses.

### Changed

- **The ladvien dependencies are crates.io versions (0.1 / 0.2), and two of this crate's own types are gone because the crate below it grew them.** `bevy_orca` 0.2 adds `Crowd` — the spatial hash, the reused scratch and the deterministic nudge every ORCA host writes for itself — so `steer_movers` now builds a `CrowdMember` per mover and calls one `solve`, and its hand-rolled O(n²) all-pairs neighbour scan is deleted. `bevy_stigmergy` 0.2 adds `CellFrame`, which deletes this crate's own `world_to_cell`, and gives `ChannelDef` the `Reflect` derive that this crate's mirror type `FieldChannel` existed to provide — so `SocialFieldSettings` now carries `frame: CellFrame` and `presence`/`commotion` as `bevy_stigmergy::ChannelDef`, and `FieldChannel` is deleted. Host-visible: `SocialFieldSettings`'s `origin`, `cell_size`, `width` and `height` fields are replaced by `frame`, and `FieldChannel` is no longer exported (`ChannelDef` is, from `bevy_stigmergy`). Every scenario, test and the `evidence` harness were re-measured on the new solver and nothing moved.

### Added

- **People speak when they have news: contact gossip.** Two people who share a place and are not otherwise exchanging now talk **when the speaker has a rumor the listener does not know**. This is the unshipped half of `how-can-two-groups-ever-meet.md`, whose own recommendation was "a 'people who share a place notice each other' system, worth having for its own sake". The earlier attempt queued a `chat` for every pair in contact and turned a village of 24 into **267 friend pairs** — 97% of every possible pair — because a `chat` is offered whether or not either person has anything to say. The fault was the gate, not the rate: `Knowledge::best_telling` only yields a rumor that is alive, whose subject is not the listener, that the teller still spreads, and that the listener does not know, so a pair with no news between them is offered nothing. On `village-evening` at the same seed the village reads **25 friends / 28 rivals / -0.052 / rumor reach 0.875** against the shipped **22 / 33 / -0.054 / 0.583**: reach up 50%, rivals down 15%, clique failure mode absent. New `Tuning` field `contact_gossip_cooldown_days`, defaulting to **2.0 game-days** — a design default set by measurement, not a paper. The cooldown is load-bearing: every telling to an *informed* listener stifles the teller (Daley & Kendall 1964, DOI 10.1038/2041118a0), so at **0.5 days** the pass burns the spreader pool faster than it spreads news and reach *falls* to **0.375**, while at **6.0 days** it changes nothing at all (0.583). The explicit `theirs.knows(rumor)` check is what keeps a contact-tell from spending a spreader on a neighbour who already knows; omitting it as "redundant" produced the 0.375 regression, which only the reach panel of the evidence plot showed. Civil inattention is the source for acknowledging presence without special interest (Goffman 1963 via Cañigueral & Hamilton 2019, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00560); proximity as the channel is Eckland 1968 via Buss 1993 (DOI 10.1037/0033-295x.100.2.204). `tests/contact_gossip.rs` pins four properties, each verified to fail when its behaviour is removed. `rumor-crosses-a-cluster` **stays `pending`**: the gate opens on news, not distance, so two groups that never share a place never exchange, and the test asserts that so the scenario cannot be marked measured on a number nobody checked.

- **`--friend-affinity` in the evidence harness, and the measurement it made possible: the friendship bar is not the lever — the asymptote is.** Sweeping `Tuning::friend_affinity` from 0.4 to 0.8 moves a 24-person village from **22 to 16** friend-ties, because affinity reaches **1.0** for anyone in contact, so every bar below 1.0 is eventually passed. A run four times longer than any scenario's shows the count is **not a runaway** either: it saturates at **122** friend-ties by game-day 24 (44% of all 276 possible pairs) and decays to 109 by day 57. The problem is a saturating system with a **high asymptote**, and the asymptote is wherever affinity settles. Capping affinity to `[-0.35, 0.35]` — *below* the `0.4` bar, so contact alone cannot make a friend — is the **first shape in six attempts** to bound the network without inverting it: friends **22 -> 15**, rivals **33 -> 18**, holding at three seeds. It costs mood (**-0.054 -> -0.078**), and two different bars give identical mood, which isolates the cost to the clamp: `chat` and `compliment` gate on affinity at `Logistic(8.0, 0.4)` and `Logistic(8.0, 0.7)`, so a lower ceiling means fewer contacts and fewer `Liking` exchanges. One trap is recorded: scaling by the asymptote (`delta · (1 - |a|/K)`) is **asymmetric** — a negative step reaches the outer clamp while a positive one decays — and measured as rivals **rising 33 -> 48**; a plain `clamp(-K, K)` bounds both. The model change was **not** shipped: bounding the network and losing mood is a trade a host should choose, and `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md` now carries every number needed to choose.


- **Six rates that were literals are now on `Tuning`, because the README promised they already were.** `README.md` states that *"every rate is a named field on `Tuning`"*; it was false in two places, both host-facing. `TieLabel::classify` decided friend, rival and stranger from the literals `0.4`, `0.3`, `0.2` and `0.1` — documented *in the same function* as design defaults with no source — and `resolve_interactions` took a literal `0.05` of affinity from a host whose approach was refused. They are now `friend_affinity`, `friend_familiarity`, `rival_affinity`, `rival_familiarity`, `stranger_familiarity` and `rejection_affinity_penalty`, with `classify` taking `&Tuning`. **Defaults equal the literals, so nothing moves:** 67 199 of 67 199 `village-evening` series rows are identical and the village stays at 22 friends / 33 rivals — this is a surface change, and the test proves `classify` reads the fields by raising `friend_affinity` to 0.9 and watching a tie demote from `Friend` to `Acquaintance`. Made visible at a moment when it matters: measured, `friend_familiarity = 0.3` is satisfied by **0.907** among friend-ties and **0.908** among the rest, so that term discriminates nothing at all — a fact only readable in the source until now. See `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md` for why the friendship question itself is still open.


- **A scenario marked `measured` whose observable nothing asserted, and which two documents described differently.** `village-evening`'s observable says a rumor planted at tick 300 is *"told to at least one other person"*; `grep` across `tests/` found nothing asserting any part of it, so four iterations of model change passed through it unverified and every reading had been by eye. Running it at ten seeds found the condition failing at two — and the cause was not the gossip model. `scenarios.yaml` says tick **300**, while `examples/evidence.rs::plant_at` scales the authored 560 by `seconds_per_day / 4` and plants at **8 960**: thirty times later, past the village's busiest period, where one rumor competes with more of the ~39 it creates each day. Measured, the condition holds at **7 of 7 seeds planted at 300** (reaching 2-8 villagers) and at 8 of 10 at 8 960. Separately, `known_by` counted `PersonIndex`, which includes the player — a `Person` without `Autonomous` that never gossips, so a rumor reaching only the player had died while satisfying the observable. `tests/village_evening.rs` now asserts it across seven seeds, counting villagers only; both plant ticks are stated in the scenario file and in the harness, with the measurement that shows they disagree in outcome.

- **The positivity offset: an idle person now rests mildly pleased.** The mood model had the *negativity bias* and not the *positivity offset*, and that absence is why pleasure could never be positive. Cacioppo, Cacioppo & Gollan 2013 (DOI `10.1017/S0140525X13002537`) name both: *"the intercept for the positive activation function (i.e., the approach motivation at zero input) is higher than the intercept for the negative activation function (producing the positivity offset), and the gain for the negative activation function is higher than for the positivity activation function (producing the negativity bias)."* The crate had the gain — a landed insult pays Anger 0.5 against a compliment's Joy 0.3, and distress outnumbers joy 3:1 by count — and no intercept, so `Mood::step` returned pleasure to a personality baseline of exactly **zero** for a sampled personality and its ceiling was zero. Measured on `village-evening`, pleasure's maximum over every person and every tick was exactly **+0.000**. `Tuning::positivity_offset` (default 0.1, magnitude a design default) adds the intercept to the resting point: max pleasure **+0.000 → +0.0155** at the default and **+0.0476** at 0.3, mean pleasure **-0.154 → -0.122**, with needs unchanged. Arousal and dominance have no offset and still return to the bare baseline. `positivity_offset: 0.0` reproduces the old behaviour exactly, and a test pins that.

- **`Obstacle`: a way to say a thing is solid.** `steer_movers` passed `&[]` for ORCA's wall list, and `src/steering.rs` said so outright — *"the crate knows no level geometry, so walls are not passed to ORCA; games with walls drive movement themselves."* A host with a tavern therefore had no way to say it was solid, and a person's `Transform` ended inside it: measured on the demo scene, a walker crossed a block whose face was 1.5 m from its centre and came within **0.01 m** of the centre. Attach `Obstacle { radius }` to anything round and solid and it becomes a hard wall half-plane in the solver, with `push_out_of_obstacles` resolving contact — which `bevy_orca` explicitly leaves to the caller. Same walker with an `Obstacle` stops at **2.35 m**. The README clips showed the old behaviour as half-bodies emerging from buildings.

- **The six README clips were rebuilt, and every one of them had been showing the wrong thing.** `village.gif` replaces `hero.gif`. Found by inspecting frames 0/120/239 of each: `needs` ringed `rest` while captioned food (`NEED_CLIP` was 1; food is 2), `opinions` drew `kindness` while captioned honesty (`OpinionBar(0)`; honesty is 1), and `village` drew halos for `RumorId(0)` — the first rumor of the run, not the planted one — so the clip showed **no** rumor while its HUD counted 10/24. The correct rumor function already existed and was never called. Also: villagers spawned **inside** 3 m buildings; a place's 1.5 m half-width equalled `interaction_radius`, so "arrived" required standing inside the block and nobody ever walked; at `seconds_per_day = 8` a 3 m walk cost 6.4 game-hours against a 0.3-hour chat; all twenty-four villagers shared one `Home`; faction teal and blue were 0.19 apart and quantized into one colour; and the HUD plate covered a third of the frame. Each clip now differs by what it draws: rumor halos matching the counter, mood as green/amber/red halos of varying size, opinion as radial bars whose colour is the sign and length the magnitude, hunger as halos that shrink across the clip, ties as a readable graph, and three factions in orange, green and violet. `tools/gif.py` replaces `tools/gif.sh` — Pillow only, because neither ffmpeg nor ImageMagick is installed on every host and the shell script failed *after* the frames were rendered.

### Changed

- **Both remedies for the friendship clamp's opinion coupling were measured and are dead; the lever is the anchor pull, and lowering it means tuning a cited model.** The previous entry left two options: a longer `ticks:` in the opinion scenario, or a compensating `influence_mu`. Neither works. Under the clamp, opinion spread **asymptotes**: 18 000 and 24 000 ticks both give **0.4036**, so no duration reaches the 0.3405 bar and the scenario can never pass. Doubling `influence_mu` to 0.6 made it **worse** (0.3813 -> 0.4337), because convergence has **two** affinity-scaled paths — Deffuant (`0.5*trust + 0.5*max(0, affinity)`, 0.425 clamped against 0.645 unclamped) and conformity (`max(0.1, affinity + 1)`, **1.35** against **1.79**) — and `mu` scales only the first, so raising it skews the ratio instead of restoring the total. The actual mechanism is that **`anchor_pull` is the only term not affinity-weighted**: `anchor_step = rate * stubbornness * (anchor - x)`. The equilibrium is `social pull / anchor pull`, so clamping halves the numerator and moves every person toward their own anchor — which predicts the stable ~0.40 asymptote exactly. Lowering `anchor_pull` does make the scenario pass, but only at 0.04-0.045, a **2.5x cut** to the Friedkin-Johnsen term, and non-monotonically (0.03 fails again at 0.3720). With all four changes the village reads **16 friends / 10 rivals / -0.052** against the shipped **22 / 33 / -0.054**: rivals down 70%, mood unchanged. It also fails `trust_rises` (accept 0.538 against 0.542) although mean trust rises, because `AcceptDef` centres on trust 0.5. **`affinity` has four readers** — tie labels, Deffuant influence, conformity and acceptance — in three different formulas, three of them invisible from the scenarios the clamp targets. That is why nine attempts failed. Three options are recorded in `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`, with the recommendation to never tune the anchor.

- **The friendship clamp's real cost is the opinion model, and the mood cost turns out to be a curve/gate mismatch that can be repaired.** Last iteration attributed the clamp's cost (`-0.054 -> -0.078`) to fewer affinity-gated contacts. That is true but not the cause: `compliment` gates on `Affinity` at `Logistic(8.0, 0.7)`, which scores **0.057** at the clamp ceiling of `0.35` against **0.673** at the unclamped mean of `0.79` — compliments are **12x less likely**, purely from where the gate sits relative to the ceiling. Moving the gate to `Logistic(16.0, 0.25)` recovers mood to **-0.045**, better than the `-0.054` baseline. The friend bar must also move under the ceiling (`0.4` is unreachable at `0.35`), so with `friend_affinity` at `0.30` and a trust second input the village reads **16 friends / 10 rivals / -0.045** against the shipped **22 / 33 / -0.054** — better on all three axes. **It still cannot ship:** `tests/opinion_convergence.rs` fails, because Deffuant influence is **weighted by tie strength**, so halving the affinity ceiling halves how fast opinions converge — spread falls **25%** where the scenario asserts **33%**. `affinity` is therefore shared between liking *and* influence, and a scenario's tick count is silently a statement about that rate. None of the six earlier attempts found this, because none changed the affinity *scale*; they changed rates, gates or the accumulation shape. Recorded with three options in `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`.

- **Five mechanisms have now been tested against "everyone becomes friends", and none works — with one cause.** The thresholds in `TieLabel::classify` were the previous iteration's hypothesis. Measured, they do not discriminate: friend-ties average affinity **0.792** against **-0.223** for the rest, but familiarity is **0.907 against 0.908** — no separation at all — while trust separates sevenfold (**0.480 against 0.068**). Gating `Friend` on trust therefore looked right and did nothing: at `trust >= 0.6` the village is unchanged at **22 friends / 33 rivals**, and with a contact-triggered exchange at 3 h it is **267 / 0**, because `chat` pays `trust: 0.05` per accepted exchange so contact talk adds **+0.4 trust per day** and trust saturates exactly as affinity does. Saturating all three accumulators (affinity, trust, respect) gives 23 / 43 alone and **268 / 0** with contact, because saturation bounds at **1.0** and every threshold is at or below **0.4**. The finding that covers all five failures: **no tie variable can sit below a threshold for a pair that has had contact** — all four accumulate, every exchange supplies all of them, so any *level*-based gate is passed by contact volume. Reverted; the crate is unchanged. The only untested shape is an asymptote **below** the threshold, recorded in `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md`.

- **The recommended fix for "everyone becomes friends" was tested and does not work.** `docs/loop/needs-decision/friendship-has-no-diminishing-return.md` recommended saturating interaction affinity — `affinity + gain · (1 − |affinity|)`, the form exposure already uses — on the reasoning that the *n*-th conversation should be worth less than the first. Built and measured on `village-evening`: **saturation alone** is nearly neutral (friends **22 → 22**, rivals 33 → 38), but it does not survive the mechanism that caused the problem — with a contact-triggered exchange at 3 h it gives **260 friends and 0 rivals**, against **267 and 0** without it. Both curves cross the `0.4` Friend threshold, and both cross it within 45 exchanges, so a saturating gain changes **when** friendship happens and not **whether**. Reverted; the crate is unchanged at friends 22 / rivals 33. The recommendation is retracted, and the decision file now ranks the untested shapes instead — recommending a **gate on trust** next, because trust already exists in the crate and moves far more slowly than affinity, which is the only property that can bound a friendship that contact alone would otherwise create. No paper in the corpus supplies the saturating shape: the closest, Reiter et al. 2023 (DOI `10.31234/osf.io/34vnx`), is about developmental trajectories of trust, not per-exchange diminishing returns. This is the **third** mechanism to fail on one problem, after a `help` activity (rivals 44 → 0) and a contact exchange (friends 22 → 267).

- **A contact-triggered exchange was built, measured and reverted: it turns a village into a clique.** `resolve_interactions` fires an exchange when a person *arrives* at an activity, once, so two people who arrive hours apart share a place and not a moment — measured, two co-located people produce **30** exchanges in 20 000 ticks and `rumor-crosses-a-cluster`'s two groups share a market for **434** ticks and exchange **nothing**. A `talk_in_contact` system walking the `Perception.contact` neighbourhood that `expose_contacts` already computes fixed that (**30 -> 89**, cooldown verified tick by tick at 1 920 ticks for 3 game-hours) — and **broke the village**: friends **22 -> 267** and rivals **33 -> 0** at a 3-hour cooldown, 182/3 at 12 hours and 72/12 at 48. **267 friend pairs in a village of 24 is 97% of all possible pairs**, because the contact pass adds **13 742** requests over 17 game-days against roughly 7 500 baseline interactions: in a village everyone is always in contact, so any rate high enough to bridge a 40 m gap is high enough to make everyone friends. The mechanism conflates *meeting* with *befriending* — two people sharing a tavern are aware of each other, they do not become friends by standing. Reverted; the crate is at friends 22 / rivals 33. The measurement and three options are in `docs/loop/needs-decision/how-can-two-groups-ever-meet.md`. This is the second mechanism to produce a clique from a plausible local rule — a `help` activity did the same two iterations earlier (rivals 44 -> 0) — so the village's tie formation is far more sensitive to contact volume than its rates suggest.

- **`rumor-crosses-a-cluster` fails, and the cause is arrival timing rather than distance.** The scenario's observable says a rumor crosses to 40% of the second group when one person from each is *"brought together once"*, and it has been `pending` since November with nothing checking it. Measured, the rumor reaches **0 of 6** at 64 s/day and **0 of 6** at 240, over the same game-days. Six layers were checked separately and **all work** — steering walks at 1.4 m/s, **359 948** person-ticks of movement, a bridge place chosen for **36 489**, **6 720** ticks arrived, **434 ticks** with both groups arrived, and one person seeing two of the other group at **2.89 m** — while cross-group interactions are **0**. The cause: **an exchange fires on *arrival*, once.** Two people placed together in the smallest case give **39 interactions** over 20 000 ticks, so the mechanism is sound, but a pair arriving at tick 100 and tick 500 shares 400 ticks of standing together and shares no arrival — and they arrive hours apart by construction, because a 20 m walk is **5.4 game-hours** against a **0.75-hour** activity. This is `travel-time-in-two-clocks.md` producing an observable outcome for the first time. `tests/cluster_reach.rs` measures the second group of six (not `rumor_reach`, which divides by all twelve) and **asserts the current failure**, so marking the scenario `measured` requires deleting that line deliberately. Two fixes were tried and measured before the cause was found: a crossroads carrying `gathering` left the reach at 0.167 because `Place` targets pick the *nearest* match and each group's own hearth is 2 m away; a market carrying a tag the hearths lack was chosen for 36 489 ticks and still reached 0 of 6.

- **`need-cycle` asked a need to starve that the rates make impossible, so it had been `pending` since November.** Its observable said *"`rest` and `food` fall and rise again over a game-day: each crosses below 0.35 and recovers above 0.7"*. Measured per person at the scenario's own settings (64 s/day, 80 000 ticks), `rest` never crosses 0.35 at **any** seed and cannot: `sleep` restores `0.15/game-hour x 8 h = 1.20` against a `rest` decay of `0.70/day`, a net **+0.50 per sleep**, so it ratchets up until sleeping stops being chosen and never needs to run low. `food` cycles **8 of 8 people** at both seeds because eating supplies `1.2 x 0.75 h = 0.90` against an exactly equal `0.9/day` decay, and `fun` cycles **8 and 6 of 8** — which the observable's own correction (*"only `food` cycles"*) denied, and which the test printed without asserting while its name said `only_food_cycles`. The observable is restated to what the model does — every need is met and re-met, none left falling with no activity to satisfy it — the per-seed cycle counts are recorded rather than asserted as constants, the test asserts the restated property, and the scenario is `measured` for the first time. The rates were **not** retuned to fit the old claim: the decay is cited (Maslow 1943) and the rates are data a host sets.

- **A deed seen by a crowd now costs each witness less.** `witness_reaction` applied `trust_gain * harm` at full magnitude to **every** witness within perception, so a deed's total cost grew linearly with the crowd — while a trust *gain* from an interaction could only ever reach the two parties. One `chat` was `+0.10` in total against an insult seen by three people at `-0.42`, and `village-evening`'s mean trust sat at 0.345 against the 0.5 that `AcceptDef` treats as neutral. `Tuning::witness_dilution` (default 0.5) divides the cost by `witnesses ^ dilution`: measured, one witnessed insult costs each witness **0.0800** with one bystander and **0.0431** with five, where before it was 0.0800 in both. Trust at an exchange **0.345 -> 0.435**, accept rate **0.533 -> 0.558**; `village-evening` mean pleasure **-0.127 -> -0.046** at seed 42 and **-0.110 -> -0.052** at seed 99, with rivals **50 -> 34** at seed 42, while seed 7 barely moved (-0.058 -> -0.054) and seed 99's rivals rose 45 -> 47 — recorded rather than averaged away. The dilution touches the deed's *magnitude* only: affinity, respect and the emotion keep their sign and full weight. Sommerfeld, Krambeck, Semmann & Milinski 2007 (DOI `10.1073/pnas.0704598104`) measured the mechanism directly: gossip transmission has *"a dampening effect ... the more extreme a gossip was, the less impact it had on the reader"*, and believability rises with the number of sources. `witness_dilution: 0.0` restores the old behaviour.

- **A pleasant conversation now registers emotionally, for both people.** `chat` and `gossip` paid `social` to both parties and **no emotion to either**, so 43% of accepted interactions in the starter pack — the two most common — left no mark on anyone's mood, and the actor who initiated a pleasant exchange gained no affect from it in any interaction (`compliment` paid only its target). Measured over 20 000 ticks and 24 people: **1 216 refusals produced exactly 1 216 distress appraisals**, while **1 338 acceptances produced 443 positive ones**. Both interactions now pay mutual `Liking` on accept (0.2 and 0.15, design defaults): positive appraisals **443 → 1 491**, the negative:positive ratio **3.58:1 → 1.06:1**, and mean pleasure **-0.122 → -0.066**, holding at three seeds (-0.127 → -0.065 at 42, -0.110 → -0.070 at 99) with needs unchanged. At the end of a run the steady state went from Anger 14 / Distress 4 / **Joy 0 / Liking 0** to Anger 14 / Distress 25 / **Joy 22 / Liking 14**, and max pleasure from -0.047 to **+0.237**: positive affect was absent and is now present. The negativity **bias** is deliberate and unchanged — Cacioppo, Cacioppo & Gollan 2013 (DOI `10.1017/S0140525X13002537`) give the negative channel the steeper gain, so an insult still pays `Anger 0.5` against a conversation's `Liking 0.2`.

- **A refused interaction's distress is now a named `Tuning` field, and a definition's own `on_reject` replaces it.** The refusal emotion was a literal `0.3` in `exchange` — the crate's only unnamed emotion intensity, against its own rule that every rate is a named field. `Tuning::rejection_distress` names it (design default, OCC mechanism cited). More importantly, **two paths for one event** are gone: a pack's `on_reject` emotion now *replaces* the crate's rather than stacking with it. Measured at identical accept/refuse counts, mean pleasure over eight people: literal 0.3 with no `on_reject` in the pack **-0.202**; pack `on_reject 0.1` with both emitted **-0.207** — worse than doing nothing; pack `on_reject 0.1` replacing the fallback **-0.165**. The starter pack's `chat` and `gossip` gain `on_reject: (actor_emotion: Some((Distress, 0.1)))`, so a refused greeting is mildly deflating rather than a small tragedy.

- **Perception runs on a uniform grid, and the crate's own account of its scale curve was wrong.** `perceive` scanned every pair, and `docs/guide/concepts.md`, `examples/benchmark.rs` and `scenarios.yaml` all stated that this quadratic search was what a host pays for. It is now a uniform grid with one cell per `perception_radius` -- the direct reading of Ericson 2005, *Real-Time Collision Detection*, §7.1.1 for points -- filled in ascending index order so a person's neighbours come out **exactly** as the all-pairs scan produced them: 67 199 of 67 199 `village-evening` series rows are identical, and `tests/perception.rs` pins the identity across five populations and two radii. Measured: **254.5 -> 245.1 ms** a tick at 5 000 people, **14.6 -> 13.5 ms** at 1 000. **3-7%, not a rescaling** -- because the instrumented breakdown shows `perceive` is **36 ms of a ~320 ms fixed step** at 5 000 people. What actually scales is **scoring**: every `Person`-targeted activity is scored against every neighbour, and at 5 000 people on the benchmark's 40 m disc an 8 m radius genuinely holds **182** neighbours (0.995 people per m²), so a tick runs roughly `5 000 x 4 x 182 = 3.6M` consideration evaluations against 144 000 at 1 000. The docs now say that, and say that no index removes it.

### Added

The doc now says so, and states what neither rate reveals alone: `mere_exposure_per_day` and `affinity_relax_per_day` are two first-order processes on one variable, so affinity settles at `0.05 / (0.05 + 0.01) = 0.833` — **52% above the `0.4` that `TieLabel::classify` calls a Friend**, which makes proximity *sufficient* for friendship. Three sweeps are recorded there because they **disproved the hypothesis that this was the cause**: lowering exposure to 0.02, 0.01 and 0.005 moves the village's friends only **22 → 15 → 15 → 16**, and lowering `balance_rate_per_day` from 0.5 to 0.05 moves it **22 → 25**. - **A grudge was the count of offences, not the worst one — and half a villager's evening went to snubbing.** `Memories::grudge` summed `max(0, -valence) * recency` over every memory about a person and clamped at 1. The sum made the value a function of `memory_capacity`: four retained trivial slights saturated it exactly as one betrayal did, so a person's opinion of another was decided by how many small entries the buffer happened to hold. `snub`'s only consideration is that grudge and it satisfies no need, so nothing pushed back: measured on `rumor-crosses-a-cluster` (12 people), `snub` was **170 of 338** activity starts at seed 11 and 154 of 318 at seed 42, with runs of **ten** and **eleven** in a row, and the narrative read `snub, compliment, snub, compliment` all evening. - **An activity's odds no longer scale with how many targets it has.** `targets_for` gives a `Person` activity one candidate per person in perception and a `Place` activity one per matching place, and selection drew over every candidate — so an activity's selection mass was `weight · score · (number of targets)`. A host will notice that activity barks now fire as a person sets off rather than on arrival, which is the better reading of a line like "Back to it.".

The starter pack's `gossip` activity is gated on distance rather than familiarity, so a rumor can spread in one evening instead of waiting for ties, and the capture counts barks across its settle steps rather than only the recorded frames.

- **Daily life and social exchanges (`BehaviorPlugin`).** Autonomous people choose activities by compensated utility over needs, mood, personality, emotions, ties and the clock; they travel to a point, place, home or person, arrive within `interaction_radius`, satisfy needs at the definition's per-hour rate, and end an activity on duration, on a lost person target, or on a travel timeout. A social exchange runs the `requires` script, the acceptance draw, both effect sets, and on acceptance opinion influence, gossip, a contest, a judged deed, triadic closure and intergroup contact; both sides record an `Interaction` memory and gain exposure. `InteractionResolved`, `ActivityStarted` and `ActivityEnded` report it, and the trigger queue sees exchanges and activities in the same tick. See `docs/research/needs-and-utility.md` (Mark & Dill, Game AI Pro 3 ch. 13; Dill 2015, DOI 10.1201/b18373-5).

- **Examples scaffolding.** `examples/common/recorder.rs` is the headless recorder the capture example drives: no window, no winit, synchronous pipeline compilation, an off-screen render target, a hand-pumped loop with a device poll per frame, and `TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration(1/30 s)` so a captured frame is exactly one thirtieth of a second of game time. `tools/gif.sh` is the other half: a two-pass palette encode with the caption burned in.

- **Script runtime and rules (`RulesPlugin`).** The `ctx` / `fx` Rhai API over a per-tick snapshot: `ctx` reads the society as it stood at the tick start and `fx` queues writes that one exclusive system applies after `SocietySystems::Rules`. Triggers fire on `Tick`, `Hour`, `Custom` (`FireTrigger`), `TieLabelChanged`, `NormViolated`, `NeedBelow` and `RumorReach`, honouring `once` and `cooldown_hours`; a failed condition counts as false and a failed effect applies none of its actions. Every evaluation is sandboxed (10 000 operations, bounded strings, arrays and maps).

- **Dialogue (`DialoguePlugin`, feature `dialogue`).** Conversations and barks through `bevy_speech_bubbles` at its pinned rev. `TalkTo { npc, player }` opens the highest-priority conversation rule whose `if` holds against the tick-start snapshot, and is ignored while a conversation is open. Node effects run their script; each finished conversation records a `Talked` memory for both people and a two-way exposure. `fx.converse` queues a `TalkTo`. `SocietyBark` lines are forwarded under the speaker key `society:<PersonId>`, which lives as long as the person. The plugin runs in `Update` because speech bubbles are frame-driven.

- **Beliefs, rumors and reputation (`BeliefsPlugin`).** Opinions per topic with bounded-confidence influence, Friedkin–Johnsen anchor pull and conformity to a strong group mean; a rumor book with the Daley–Kendall stifling rule and trust-scaled belief; and the witness, patient and actor reactions to a deed, including `NormViolated`. `PlantRumor` starts a rumor from the game or a script. See `docs/research/opinion-dynamics.md` and `docs/research/rumor-and-reputation.md` (Deffuant et al. 2000, DOI 10.1142/S0219525900000078; Daley & Kendall 1964, DOI 10.1038/2041118a0; Ohtsuki & Iwasa 2004, DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2004.06.005).

- **Engine-free model math (`bevy_society::model`).** Response curves, the Mehrabian/ALMA personality-to-mood baseline, power-law familiarity and exposure, Deffuant opinion influence with anchor pull and conformity, Daley–Kendall stifling, and triadic balance. Each formula's DOI sits in a comment beside it.

- **Relationships (`RelationshipsPlugin`).** Directed, weighted ties per person with power-law familiarity (Anderson & Schooler 1991), mere exposure (Zajonc 1968), affinity relaxation toward zero, Heider structural balance over sampled triads, a Dunbar-style cap on ties, and labels (stranger, acquaintance, friend, rival) with a `TieLabelChanged` message. See `docs/research/relationships.md` (Heider 1946, DOI 10.1080/00223980.1946.9917275; Dunbar 1992, DOI 10.1016/0047-2484(92)90081-J).

- **Groups and status (`GroupsPlugin`).** Faction memberships with an in-group bias, standing as respect conferred by familiar persons, logistic contests weighted by dominance and standing, intergroup contact (Pettigrew & Tropp 2006), and per-faction cohesion and regard each tick. See `docs/research/groups-and-status.md` (Tajfel et al. 1971, DOI 10.1002/ejsp.2420010202; Bonabeau et al. 1995, DOI 10.1016/0378-4371(95)00064-e).

- **Social fields (`fields` feature).** Per-cell crowd presence and commotion grids plus one presence grid per faction, deposited in person order and evaporated per game-day on `bevy_stigmergy`. Schelling's tolerance input is the `OwnShare` field (own / (own + other)), and `Gradient` activity targets follow the field toward or away from a cluster. See `docs/research/groups-and-status.md` (Schelling 1971, DOI 10.1080/0022250X.1971.9989794).

- **Mind (`MindPlugin`).** Emotions (OCC kinds with ALMA PAD values) are appraised by `Appraise` messages and decay with a 0.1 game-day half-life. Mood is pulled toward the intensity-weighted emotion centre and returns to a personality baseline (Mehrabian 1996; Gebhard 2005), with Hatfield-style contagion from affinity-weighted contacts. Needs decay at `decay_per_day`. See `docs/research/personality-and-mood.md` (DOI 10.1080/00049539608259510).
